01StructureBody shell+

A low, purposeful shape.

The body provides the passenger compartment and attachment points for the vehicle’s systems. Its exterior surfaces also manage airflow, packaging and visibility.

How it works

The floor-mounted battery and the body are different assemblies. The simplified model here is an explainer, not a structural repair drawing. Do not infer a structural-battery design or repair method from its shape.

  • Body structure and battery casing are distinct references
  • Dimensions and equipment vary by build
  • Use repair documentation for actual construction

Tesla service manual: Model 3 systems →

02ExteriorGlass and glazing+

An open cabin, with layers of engineering.

Glazing brings light into the cabin while seals, coatings and laminated constructions influence noise and thermal comfort.

How it works

Acoustic glazing and solar control address different physical effects. The appearance of a glass roof does not establish the specification of every pane or every trim.

  • Equipment depends on the vehicle and market
  • Sunlight and cabin temperature are separate issues
  • The 3D glass is an illustrative material

Tesla service manual: Model 3 systems →

03ExteriorLighting+

See the road. Be seen.

Headlights and rear lamps combine road illumination with signals that communicate braking and manoeuvres to other road users.

How it works

Beam behaviour depends on fitted hardware, software and local regulations. A visible lamp shape in this 2024 model does not prove a newer lighting feature is enabled.

  • Check the controls and manual for your vehicle
  • Local rules affect available lighting functions
  • The gallery photograph identifies its model year

Tesla service manual: Model 3 systems →

04ExteriorMirrors+

A useful view beyond the body.

Exterior mirrors help the driver monitor surrounding traffic and the area alongside the car. Cameras provide additional views with different limitations.

How it works

A camera image, mirror and direct observation do not offer the same field of view. Adjust the mirrors for the driver and check visibility rather than assuming a displayed image removes blind spots.

  • Adjustment is driver-specific
  • Keep mirrors and cameras clean
  • Illustrative geometry is not a visibility measurement

Tesla Model 3 camera guidance →

05ChassisWheels and tyres+

The final connection to the road.

Tyres determine how steering, drive torque and braking forces reach the road. Wheel size and tyre specification can change efficiency and ride comfort.

How it works

Pressure, load, temperature and tyre condition affect behaviour. Larger wheels do not automatically improve every aspect of grip or comfort. Choose approved sizes and maintain the specified pressures.

  • Use the vehicle’s tyre-pressure label
  • Range figures can depend on wheel choice
  • The model is not a fitment or tyre-size guide

Tesla service manual: Model 3 systems →

06ChassisFriction brakes+

Control beyond the acceleration figure.

Disc brakes use friction to slow the wheels. Regenerative braking instead uses the drive system to recover some vehicle energy.

How it works

Energy recovery can be limited by conditions such as battery temperature and state of charge. The driver must remain prepared to use the brake pedal; a familiar lift-off response is not guaranteed in every condition.

  • Disc hardware in the model is illustrative
  • Regeneration and friction braking are different processes
  • Observe the vehicle’s warnings and service requirements

Tesla service manual: Model 3 systems →

07InteriorCabin and seating+

A space built around the drive.

Seat position, climate control, glazing and storage shape the everyday experience as much as a headline range figure.

How it works

Inspect the actual vehicle for seat, trim and rear-screen equipment. This licensed 2024 interior model does not reproduce every Standard, Premium or Performance configuration.

  • Try the driving position and rear-seat access
  • Check equipment on the ordered vehicle
  • Photographs retain their original model-year captions

Tesla UK Model 3 specifications →

08InteriorTouchscreens and controls+

Software meets physical controls.

The touchscreen presents navigation, charging information and many vehicle settings. Other functions use steering-wheel or dedicated physical controls.

How it works

Menus and hardware can change with software and production updates. Check the vehicle itself rather than using this 2024 render as a menu or screen-size reference.

  • The model illustrates an earlier cabin
  • Current trim specifications are a separate reference
  • Use your vehicle’s manual for operating instructions

Tesla US Model 3 specifications →

09EnergyHigh-voltage battery+

Energy stored low in the car.

The traction battery stores energy for propulsion and other high-voltage loads. Its management system monitors operation while thermal management helps maintain suitable conditions.

How it works

Nominal capacity, usable energy, cell chemistry and supplier are different attributes. A published number for one pack is not a universal Model 3 specification; this explorer does not assign one battery capacity to the whole range.

  • Battery capacity is not inferred from advertised range
  • State of charge is different from charging power
  • The pack shape and internal layout are illustrative

Tesla service manual: Model 3 systems →

10PowertrainRear drive unit+

Electrical energy into motion.

The rear drive unit combines an electric motor with reduction gearing to drive the rear wheels. Power electronics control the electrical supply.

How it works

Torque at the motor and torque at the wheel are different quantities. Acceleration also depends on tyres, vehicle mass, traction, available battery power and conditions.

  • RWD and AWD references are kept separate
  • No motor output is inferred from the 3D shape
  • The internal geometry is educational

Tesla service manual: Model 3 systems →

11PowertrainFront drive unit+

An additional driven axle on AWD cars.

Dual-motor versions use a front drive unit as well as a rear unit. A rear-wheel-drive vehicle does not acquire a front motor because it appears in this generic exploded model.

How it works

The vehicle manages torque between driven axles according to operating conditions. This model shows an illustrative AWD architecture rather than identifying the hardware of a specific vehicle.

  • Shown for the AWD architecture
  • Not fitted to a RWD configuration merely because it is visible here
  • Exact hardware requires vehicle-specific documentation

Tesla UK Model 3 specifications →

12PowertrainPower electronics+

Control the motor, not just the current.

An inverter controls the electrical power delivered to an electric motor. The traction battery supplies DC while the motor drive requires controlled electrical waveforms.

How it works

During energy recovery, the system also manages electrical energy returning from the drive units. Physical arrangement and component specification depend on the installed drive system.

  • This is a functional illustration
  • DC and controlled motor power are different stages
  • High-voltage components are not owner-serviceable

Tesla service manual: Model 3 systems →

13EnergyHeat pump and thermal system+

Move heat where it is useful.

A heat pump transfers heat between parts of the thermal system. Vehicle thermal management also manages the needs of the cabin, battery and powertrain.

How it works

Moving heat can reduce the electrical energy required for cabin warming compared with creating all that heat resistively. Actual efficiency depends on conditions, temperature and operating mode.

  • A diagram is not a refrigerant routing plan
  • Battery preparation can use energy before a stop
  • No fixed winter efficiency benefit is promised

Tesla heat-pump service reference →

14EnergyLow-voltage system+

The supporting electrical network.

A separate low-voltage system powers control electronics and other vehicle functions. It works alongside the high-voltage traction system.

How it works

Low-voltage battery type and service procedures can differ across vehicles. Use the instructions for the installed system instead of assuming every Model 3 has the same nominal voltage or chemistry.

  • Low voltage does not mean every task is safe to perform
  • Follow the vehicle-specific service instructions
  • No universal replacement specification is stated here

Tesla low-voltage battery service guidance →

15ChassisFront suspension+

Manage the contact patch.

The front suspension supports the car and controls wheel motion while the steering system sets wheel direction. Springs, dampers and links have different jobs.

How it works

Springs support load and store energy; dampers control motion. Geometry, alignment, tyres and settings affect how the complete system responds. The simplified components are not dimensional engineering data.

  • Alignment is a service setting, not a styling feature
  • Wheel travel is not shown to scale
  • Performance equipment varies by trim

Tesla service manual: Model 3 systems →

16ChassisRear suspension+

Stability through controlled movement.

The rear suspension supports the body while allowing the wheels to move with the road. Links locate the wheels and dampers manage their motion.

How it works

Changes in load or tyre specification can affect the way a car rides and responds. A visually similar assembly is not proof of identical damping, geometry or parts across trims.

  • The assembly is illustrative
  • Do not infer interchangeable parts
  • Use the correct service and alignment information

Tesla service manual: Model 3 systems →

17EnergyCharging inlet+

One connection, two charging paths.

The inlet connects the vehicle to compatible charging equipment. AC charging and DC fast charging feed the vehicle through different electrical paths.

How it works

For AC, an onboard charger converts supplied AC into DC. A compatible DC fast charger supplies DC through the vehicle’s managed charging system. Plug shape, adapters and capabilities depend on the market and vehicle.

  • AC input is not the same as DC peak power
  • The gallery shows an older European inlet
  • Check cable and adapter compatibility for your car

Tesla Model 3 charging instructions →

18Driver assistanceCameras+

Inputs, not a promise of autonomy.

Cameras provide views used by vehicle features and driver-assistance systems. Dirt, obstruction, weather and visibility can affect the information available.

How it works

A camera shown in the model does not establish software entitlement or regulatory availability. The consumer supervised system still requires an attentive driver; use the FSD tracker for country evidence.

  • Keep the relevant camera areas unobstructed
  • Feature availability is vehicle- and market-specific
  • No autonomous capability is inferred from this model

Tesla Model 3 camera guidance →

19InteriorCabin climate system+

Comfort is an energy system too.

Fans, ducts, filters and thermal equipment move and condition air for the cabin. Demisting and passenger comfort place different demands on the system.

How it works

Preconditioning while connected to power can prepare the vehicle before a journey. The energy used by climate control is part of overall vehicle consumption, not a separate free resource.

  • Set conditions for passengers and visibility
  • Filters and airflow need maintenance
  • No fixed climate-energy penalty is assumed

Your assumptions. Transparent maths.

Put a journey
into perspective.

Explore energy and cost using your own assumptions. This is an energy-equivalent illustration, not a route or charging-stop prediction.

Choose your assumptions, then calculate.

Driving energy = distance × consumption. Energy drawn = driving energy ÷ (1 − loss). Cost = drawn energy × price. Charging time = drawn energy ÷ assumed average power. No battery capacity, starting charge, arrival charge, weather or stops are inferred. Your car’s route planner is the practical guide.

01

Arrive prepared.

Navigate to the charging stop in the car so it can prepare the battery where supported. A cold pack can limit charging power.

02

Use the right limit.

Follow the charging limit recommended by your car or app. Battery chemistry and vehicle instructions vary; one rule does not fit every Model 3.

03

Read the whole session.

The advertised DC peak is a maximum, not a session average. Charging usually slows as state of charge rises.
