The reading guide

A useful record
needs clear boundaries.

This is an editorial, source-linked collection. It tracks the particular statements below, rather than every promise, product or corporate result.

01

Read the original wording.

Each entry identifies its speaker, statement date and source. Exact-quote and paraphrase labels are preserved from the existing audit; a secondary report is labelled by its publisher, not presented as a Tesla, SpaceX or xAI statement.

02

Keep the target’s precision.

Month-only and year-only dates stay broad. Some stored deadlines are editorial cutoff interpretations of wording such as “a few months.” The displayed target label, original wording and interpretation note stay together. A broad target never becomes a precise live countdown.

03

Separate evidence from inference.

Recorded evidence, revisions, outcomes and review dates have different roles. A demonstration is not general customer availability. An evidence entry may confirm only one geography or programme milestone. Missing evidence is not proof of failure.

What the status labels mean.

Delivered

The existing editorial record judges the stated scope delivered by its recorded target.

Delivered late

The existing record judges the stated scope delivered after its recorded target.

In progress

The saved review leaves the claim open. If its window later passes, the interface requests a review rather than automatically reclassifying the claim.

Missed

The saved editorial review judges that the stated target was not met. Later revisions remain visible rather than erasing the original statement.

Revised

The record treats the goal as changed, undated or otherwise outside a simple deadline judgment.

Source dates are not live verification.

The dataset is dated 22 Oct 2026. Every claim retains its individual last-verified date, which may be older. The presentation guide was reviewed 26 Oct 2026 to clarify date precision; that does not re-verify any claim or change its status.

The next-window selection refreshes in the browser using the device’s current date. Claim classifications change only through an editorial source review and an update to the underlying data.
