SpaceX · Starship

Why does Starship use hot staging?

Ship lights while it is still on Super Heavy. The stack never coasts. That is the whole point — and why the ring looks like it is on fire. First tried on Flight 2 (18 Nov 2023). Flight 14 staged at T+2:22.

No coast

Cold staging cuts the booster, waits, then lights the upper stage. Hot staging never drops the thrust. For a heavy stack, that wait is expensive.

More payload

Continuous acceleration means less gravity loss. SpaceX’s published reason is performance.

The hardware cost

The ring has to survive Ship lighting in the booster’s plume. Flight 2 proved the staging. Both vehicles still died afterwards.

SpaceX · Super Heavy

How does a booster catch work?

The booster flies back to the tower it left. Chopsticks close on the pins. Flight 5 (13 Oct 2024, B12) proved it. Everything since is the same idea, faster. Flight 15 is the first serious attempt to catch a Ship.

No legs

A Super Heavy that lands on legs needs mass and a huge pad. A catch puts that mass in the tower you already built.

Catch or splash

If the approach is off, SpaceX diverts to a controlled splashdown. Missing is allowed. Hitting the tower wrong is not.

Ship is next

Flight 15 is the first serious attempt to catch a Ship. Same chopsticks idea, harder timing.

What changed between FSD releases?

Same brand, three stacks. US HW4 has Collision Evasion. HW3 is on Lite. Europe is still Assisted Driving Basic on 14.2. The driver still supervises — SAE Level 2. Pick two stacks.

    FSD (Supervised) is called Tesla Assisted Driving in Europe. Dispatch

  • 6 Oct 2026 — 14.3.11 + Lite

    HW4 gets Collision Evasion. HW3 gets Lite parking. Europe stays on 14.2. Software tracker

  • 10 Oct 2026 — 14.3.12

    Same notes as 14.3.11, on the 2026.38 cabin branch. Early US wave. Global tracker

FSD Supervised is SAE Level 2. Not Tesla’s official comparison. Version notes from the global tracker and software tracker.
