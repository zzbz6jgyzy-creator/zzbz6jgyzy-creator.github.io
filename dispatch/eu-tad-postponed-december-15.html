Tesla · News · 25 Nov 2026

The EU has postponed its Tesla Assisted Driving meeting to December 15. A rejection would invalidate national approvals already granted

A European Commission spokesperson told Reuters on Wednesday that the meeting expected to consider bloc-wide approval of Tesla Assisted Driving will now take place on December 15. Reuters published the report at 5:46am ET (2:46am PT). The Commission did not give a reason. I went through the Reuters account, the voting rule, and the national approvals we have already covered, including Italy earlier this month.

What was postponed

The meeting is of the Technical Committee for Motor Vehicles, known as the TCMV. Reuters, in a dispatch by Marie Mannes, says the committee had been expected to consider Tesla's bid, and potentially vote on it, at its December meeting. No vote was held at two earlier meetings, after a preliminary approval from the Netherlands in April.

The Commission spokesperson is the source for the new date of December 15. Reuters does not quote a longer explanation, and I could not find an official Tesla, Tesla Europe, or Elon Musk post on the delay in a search of those accounts after the report landed.

What Tesla Assisted Driving is

Tesla Assisted Driving is the name used in Europe for the supervised system sold in the United States as Full Self-Driving. Reuters says the name was changed as a result of concerns from regulators. The product is a so-called hands-off, eyes-on system: it can steer, accelerate and brake, and the driver has to stay in constant supervision.

That limit matters, because the European label still gets talked about as if it were driverless. When Italy approved the system earlier this month, Tesla's own Italian product page said the features "require active driver supervision and don't make the car autonomous." That is the English rendering I used in our Italy piece, from the page as fetched on 20 November. The driver stays responsible for the car.

The national rollouts already underway

Reuters says nearly a dozen countries have followed the Dutch lead, with Italy the most populous to begin a rollout. That lines up with the count we have already published. The Netherlands' RDW gave a provisional approval on 10 April 2026. Other member states have been recognising that approval one at a time. Finland was the 11th, on 11 November. Italy, on 20 November, was the 12th, a figure that also matches Il Sole 24 Ore. Tesla Europe posted that morning that the system was "now approved in Italy" and that "rollout begins soon." Italy's infrastructure minister, Matteo Salvini, said the ministry had given a provisional go-ahead and that Italy was moving ahead of the EU recognition process, which he described as "attualmente in fase di definizione" (still being worked out). I quoted that post in the earlier Italy piece.

Those national steps let Tesla offer the supervised system in those countries, in some cases before Brussels has voted. They are recognitions of the Dutch provisional approval, which is why a later EU-level rejection would cut across all of them at once. Reuters singles Italy out as the most populous country to start a rollout. It does not say that every country in the "nearly a dozen" has already put the software in customers' cars, and I am not filling that gap.

What a Brussels yes or no would do

EU-wide approval, Reuters notes, requires backing from at least 15 of the 27 member states, representing 65% of the bloc's population. That is the ordinary qualified-majority rule. A yes would give Tesla a single, bloc-wide path. A rejection at EU level, Reuters says, would invalidate the national approvals already granted.

That second sentence is the one that makes this delay more than a calendar change. If the committee later votes no, the national recognitions already in force, including Italy's, would be pulled. When I added Italy to the ledger on 20 November, the 12 countries then in the national column held about 29% of the EU's population, by my calculation from Eurostat's 1 January 2026 figures. That is well short of 65%. Germany, France and Spain were not in that column.

The committee has already met twice without voting, and Wednesday's report does not say a vote is locked in for December 15. It says the meeting expected to consider the system has been moved to that date.

How the date moved

December 1 had already been a later date than the two earlier meetings that ended without a vote. The meeting had originally been scheduled for that day, the Dutch regulator RDW told Reuters. Belgium's Flanders transport ministry initially said it had been moved to December 7, before the Commission clarified that it would instead take place on December 15. RDW, Flanders and the Commission each reported a date. The date that now matters is the Commission's: December 15.

No official reason was attached to the slip. I am not going to invent one. Tesla has wanted a bloc-wide decision for months, and several smaller member states plus Italy have already moved on their own. The committee can still approve the system, reject it, or discuss it again without a vote, as it has done before.

My read

The national rollouts are real, and they are still legally fragile, because a later no in Brussels would unwind them. A two-week delay does not decide the system. It does push any decision closer to the end of the year, and it leaves drivers in the countries that have already said yes waiting on a vote that has now been put off twice before.

Would you approve Tesla Assisted Driving across the EU now, or wait even if that puts the existing national rollouts at risk?

— Alex R
