SpaceX · AI · 18 Nov 2026

Nvidia's first results without Huang say Vera Rubin is "coming online soon" at SpaceXSI

Nvidia reported $115.8 billion of revenue for its third quarter on Tuesday and guided $130 billion for the next one. Its press release names SpaceX's AI unit twice: Vera Rubin NVL72 racks are "coming online soon at SpaceXSI," and Nvidia cites "the continued buildout of SpaceXSI's infrastructure." Three months ago the same release only had SpaceX's AI arm down for Vera CPUs.

The numbers

Nvidia released results for the quarter ended 25 October after the market closed on Tuesday, 17 Nov, and held its analyst call at 5 p.m. ET (2 p.m. PT). Revenue was $115.8 billion, up 20% on the quarter and 103% on a year earlier. Data center revenue was $107.6 billion. GAAP net income came in at $65.3 billion, or $2.70 a share.

In August Nvidia had guided $108.0 billion for this quarter, so it came in about $7.8 billion (7%) above its own forecast. For the fourth quarter, which has an extra week, it expects $130.0 billion, plus or minus 3%, and assumes no data center compute revenue from China.

What Nvidia said about SpaceX, then and now

This was Nvidia's first report since Jensen Huang resigned as CEO on 11 Nov. Colette Kress, the CFO, is running the company on an interim basis, so the release and the call were hers. The table puts the August and November releases side by side.

Of the six companies named in that Vera Rubin sentence, SpaceXSI is the only one without racks running yet. "Coming online soon" is Nvidia's description of a delivery that hasn't finished, and neither SpaceX nor Elon Musk had commented on it by the time I wrote this.

"Soon" on Nvidia's side, 2027 on SpaceX's

Nvidia's wording doesn't line up neatly with SpaceX's own filing. SpaceX's $40 billion Apollo financing, which SpaceX says "will deliver the next generation of NVIDIA Vera Rubin chips," is still a commitment. Its 8-K describes a binding commitment letter signed 24 Oct, a master lease expected to close before year-end and leases that begin in the first half of 2027 as the GPUs arrive.

So the racks Nvidia calls "coming online soon" are probably not the Apollo chips, or not all of them. Nvidia doesn't say which order it means, how many racks or where they're going. Musk's last public count, on 31 Oct, was about 860,000 GB300s across SpaceXSI's Memphis buildings, all of them the previous Blackwell generation. I went through that list in my piece on Sunday.

The handshake question

That Sunday piece was about Musk's warning on 14 Nov that many of Nvidia's supply deals were "a handshake with Jensen, not a written contract" and that "at least some of those critical deals will be lost."

Kress answered the general version of that worry in her opening remarks on the call. She spoke of "supply agreements extending through to the end of the decade" and closed with "Our customers and our partners are with us," according to Investing.com's transcript. She didn't name SpaceX in either line. The written release doesn't name Huang at all. The only trace of his exit is a new line in its forward-looking-statement caveats about Nvidia's ability to "identify and integrate a new chief executive officer."

Nvidia's ties to SpaceX run in both directions. Its latest 13F, filed on Monday, showed SpaceX as its biggest stock holding, 130.5 million shares worth $19.7 billion on 30 September.

What to watch

The useful confirmation would come from SpaceX: a post from Musk or SpaceXSI showing Vera Rubin racks running, or the Apollo master lease closing before year-end as the 8-K expects. Nvidia's next scheduled update is its fourth-quarter report, due in February.

My read

A results release isn't a contract, but Nvidia chose to put SpaceXSI in the Vera Rubin sentence four days after Musk said deals could be lost. I read that as Nvidia signalling the SpaceX business is still on. Whether "soon" means December or the 2027 dates in SpaceX's filing is the part I can't tell yet. Which do you believe, Nvidia's "soon" or Musk's handshake worry? Tell me below.

— Alex R
