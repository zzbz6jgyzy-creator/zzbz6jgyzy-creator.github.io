Slovakia approves Tesla FSD (Supervised), making it nine EU countries. Here's why the map still hinges on Germany

Slovakia's Ministry of Transport has recognised the Dutch approval for Tesla's supervised driver-assist system, nine days after the minister promised it. That's four EU countries in under six weeks. But the nine "yes" countries together hold only about 14% of the EU's people, and an EU-wide approval needs 65%.

On Friday morning, Slovakia's Ministry of Transport said it has recognised the provisional type approval that the Dutch regulator RDW gave Tesla's Full Self-Driving (Supervised) in April. That means Slovak Tesla owners can legally use the system on their roads once Tesla switches it on there. Slovakia is the ninth EU country to take this step, after the Netherlands, Lithuania, Estonia, Denmark, Belgium, Slovenia, Czechia and Croatia.

The move didn't come out of nowhere. In a video on 7 October, Transport Minister Jozef Ráž said, as reported by Pravda, that the ministry would send the RDW the document needed to recognise the Dutch approval within days. It took nine.

"I'm glad that when it comes to modern technology in transport, Slovakia isn't falling behind," Ráž said in the ministry's statement, as carried by TN Live. He was just as clear about the limits: "It's not a fully autonomous system." The driver stays fully responsible, and the car monitors their attention, warning them and, if needed, switching the system off.

What Slovakia actually approved

This is a recognition, not a fresh national test. Under Article 39 of the EU's vehicle type-approval rules, the Netherlands granted Tesla a provisional approval for a technology the existing rules don't cover. Any other member state can choose to accept that approval for its own territory. That's what Slovakia did on Friday, and what the eight before it did.

The ministry still used the "Full Self-Driving (Supervised)" name, but Tesla has been rolling out a new label in Europe. As electrive reported, Tesla's European sites now call the same system "Tesla Assisted Driving," a change linked to talks with German officials. Same software, friendlier name for regulators.

As of Friday morning ET, Tesla Europe hadn't posted a rollout date for Slovak customers.

Nine countries have said yes. Together they hold about 62 million people, roughly 14% of the EU. Germany alone holds about 19%.

Two waves, then a long summer gap

Line up the recognition dates against the RDW's 10 April approval (dates from our FSD Europe tracker) and a clear pattern shows up:

  • Spring wave, 21 days: Lithuania (day 40), Estonia (day 49), Denmark (day 60) and Belgium (day 61), all between 20 May and 10 June.
  • Summer pause, 89 days: no new country from 10 June to 7 September, while the EU's vehicle committee discussed the file in June without voting.
  • Autumn wave, 39 days: Slovenia (day 150), Czechia (day 164), Croatia (day 172) and now Slovakia (day 189), all between 7 September and today.

The second wave lines up with the EU-wide vote slipping. Tesla had pointed to 6 October as a possible vote date, but Reuters reported on 25 September that the agenda listed only a "continuation of discussions," and the committee's next meeting isn't until December. With Brussels stalled, smaller countries that are keen on the tech have been going ahead one by one.

The math that actually decides Europe

An EU-wide approval at the Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles (TCMV) needs a qualified majority: at least 15 of the 27 member states, representing at least 65% of the EU's population. Using rounded population figures, here's where the yes camp stands:

  • The nine that have recognised it: about 62 million people, or roughly 14% of the EU's ~448 million. That's 9 of the 15 states needed, but barely a fifth of the population threshold.
  • Add Germany: about 33%. German Transport Minister Steffen Bilger has said he'll push for Europe-wide approval "as soon as possible," per electrive.
  • Add France, Italy and Spain too: about 72%, clearing the population bar. Even then, that's only 13 countries, still two short of the 15-state requirement.

That's the real takeaway from Friday. Small countries can keep adding legal coverage, and each one matters to the Tesla owners who live there. But national recognitions don't add up to an EU-wide yes on their own. It still comes down to a vote where Germany and France carry the weight, plus enough mid-sized countries to reach 15. Reuters reported back in June that Sweden may oppose the request, so it isn't a done deal.

What to watch next

  • Tesla's Slovak rollout. Recognition makes it legal. Customers still need Tesla to enable it, and Tesla hasn't given a date yet.
  • Country number 10. Greece has discussed fast-tracking recognition, according to the European Transport Safety Council, and Finland's regulator has been reviewing the file.
  • December's TCMV meeting. That's the earliest apparent chance for an EU-wide vote. Watch for whether a draft decision actually lands on the agenda this time.
  • Germany. Bilger backs a quick EU-wide approval. A German yes would more than double the population behind the system overnight.

My read: Slovakia is a nice win and proof the country-by-country path still works, with four approvals in under six weeks. But it's a small-country story. The big unlock for Europe is still a December vote, and that one runs through Berlin.
