United now has Starlink on nearly half its daily flights

More than 635 United jets now carry SpaceX’s satellite Wi-Fi, a quarter of a million people use it every day, and the airline has built a public tracker so you can check your own flight. Its two biggest rivals have not installed a single terminal yet.

Updated 27 Oct 2026

United Airlines says Starlink is now switched on for nearly half of its daily departures. In a statement released at 8:00am ET (5:00am PT) on Thursday, the Chicago-based carrier put the number of equipped aircraft at more than 635, spread across both its mainline jets and its United Express regional fleet.

That works out to more than 2,500 flights a day and, on average, upwards of 250,000 customers every day sitting on a plane with SpaceX’s low-orbit internet overhead. Since the rollout began, United says more than 37.7 million passengers have flown on Starlink aircraft across over 538,000 flights, and 14 million devices have connected.

A tracker for your own flight

Alongside the milestone, United launched StarlinkTracker.United.com. Enter a flight number and travel dates within three days of departure and the site tells you whether that aircraft is fitted, with updates if the plane gets swapped at the last minute. It also shows the overall fleet count, a split between regional, narrowbody and widebody aircraft, and the share of Starlink flights at each of United’s seven US hubs: Chicago, Newark, Washington D.C., San Francisco, Los Angeles, Houston and Denver. United says the stats refresh daily, and notes that aircraft assignments are usually only confirmed within 24 hours of departure.

The Wi-Fi itself is free for MileagePlus members.

Install pace and the 2027 finish line

United says it is fitting roughly 20 aircraft a week and plans to finish the entire fleet by the end of 2027. It already claims more Starlink-equipped planes than any other airline in the world.

Andrew Nocella, United’s chief commercial officer, said Starlink gives customers “even more reasons to choose United,” and that the tracker makes it easier to see which flights have it “as we quickly expand Starlink across our fleet.”

Poaching rival flyers

United is also using the Wi-Fi as a weapon. Its “Switch to Better Wi-Fi” status match, which opened on 1 October for eligible Delta SkyMiles and American AAdvantage members, has been extended to 22 October. Qualifying flyers can earn United Premier status through January 2028 if they hit a spend target, from $1,500 for Silver up to $7,000 for 1K, and take a United flight within 90 days. United says applications are running 275% above pre-campaign levels, led by New York, Los Angeles and San Francisco.

Where the competition stands

American Airlines has committed to Starlink across its whole mainline fleet of more than 1,000 aircraft, but its installations only begin in early 2027, starting with Airbus narrowbodies (American Airlines newsroom).

Delta went a different way. It signed with Amazon’s rival constellation, Amazon Leo, for an initial 500 aircraft, with installs beginning in 2028 (Delta and Amazon announcements). Delta’s current Wi-Fi, free to SkyMiles members, runs on its existing provider until then.

Put simply: by the time Delta’s first Leo-equipped jet is scheduled, United intends to be finished.

What Starlink speed actually means in the air

Traditional airline Wi-Fi leaned on satellites in geostationary orbit, roughly 36,000 km up, which adds a noticeable delay to every request, or on ground towers that drop out over oceans. Starlink satellites sit in low Earth orbit, a few hundred kilometres up, so the signal round trip is far shorter. That lower latency is what makes video calls, gaming and streaming feel normal rather than laggy.

For scale, American describes Starlink’s aviation terminal as capable of up to 1 Gbps per terminal, and Amazon quotes up to 1 Gbps down and 400 Mbps up for Delta’s planned Leo antenna. Those are per-aircraft ceilings shared by everyone on board, not what each passenger will see, and real-world speeds vary by flight.

Speculation

At about 20 installs a week, United’s numbers will keep climbing fast through the rest of this year. If the pace holds, Starlink should go from “nearly half” to a clear majority of departures well before the 2027 target. The bigger question is the status-match crowd: if those Delta and American flyers get used to Starlink, the pressure on rivals to accelerate their own timelines only grows. That is my read, not something United has said.

Sources: United Airlines press release via PR Newswire, 15 Oct 2026, 8:00am ET; American Airlines newsroom; Delta and Amazon announcements on Amazon Leo; video by United Airlines (@united).

For years airline Wi-Fi was a punchline. United just made it a reason to pick an airline.

— Alex R
