Texas weighed selling Boca Chica State Park to SpaceX. Here's how Starbase's footprint got here

A draft agenda for the Texas Parks and Wildlife Commission listed the sale of "1,300 acres" in Cameron County for a closed-door session on November 5. Two sources told Texas Monthly the land is Boca Chica State Park, the beachfront park that wraps around Starbase, and that SpaceX is the buyer. After the magazine asked about it, the state said the sale isn't on November's agenda. It didn't say the idea is dead. If it goes ahead, it would be the biggest piece of public land SpaceX has added at Boca Chica, and the report lands the same week the FAA started reviewing SpaceX's request for up to 456 Starship launches a year from the site.

Updated 27 Oct 2026

The story broke at Fri 8:59pm ET, when Texas Monthly writer Forrest Wilder posted his exclusive: "Texas has been considering selling Boca Chica State Park to Elon Musk." According to the Texas Monthly report, talks inside the commission had gone far enough that a draft agenda put the sale of "1,300 acres" in Cameron County on the closed-door executive session for its November 5 meeting. That's two days after the November 3 general election.

The buyer detail rests on anonymous sourcing. Two unnamed sources "familiar with internal discussions," Texas Monthly reported, said SpaceX is the prospective buyer and confirmed that the 1,300 acres are the state park itself. Neither SpaceX nor the state has confirmed that. After Texas Monthly asked about the meeting, Parks and Wildlife spokesperson Stephanie Garcia gave a one-line statement: "The Texas Parks and Wildlife Commission is not considering the sale of the Boca Chica property at its November meeting." By the magazine's press time she hadn't answered follow-up questions about the talks, Musk's involvement, why the agenda had apparently changed, or whether the idea has been shelved. Sources told the magazine the nine commissioners, all appointed by Governor Greg Abbott, could take it up at a later date.

What's confirmed, and what isn't

  • Confirmed on the record: Texas Parks and Wildlife says a Boca Chica sale won't be considered at the November meeting, per its statement to Texas Monthly. Its website lists that meeting for November 4–5.
  • Reported, from a document: a draft agenda, seen by Texas Monthly, listed the sale of "1,300 acres" in Cameron County for executive session on November 5.
  • Reported, from two anonymous sources: SpaceX is the buyer, and the land is Boca Chica State Park.
  • Unknown: a price, whether a sale or a swap is on the table, and whether the idea is paused or dropped. We found no public comment from SpaceX or Musk on X about the report as of Saturday morning.

43 acres in 2024. 1.9 acres in 2025. 715 federal acres last month. Now, reportedly, the whole 1,300-acre park.

How SpaceX's footprint at Boca Chica has grown

Seen alone, the report sounds sudden. Line it up with the last three years and it looks like the next step in a pattern. Here are the major public-land and access changes at Boca Chica since 2024, based on public records, court rulings and news reports:

WhenDealLand involvedOutcome

May–Jun 2025Starbase becomes a city, then Texas law HB 5246Voters created the city of Starbase on May 3, 2025. HB 5246 then moved the power to approve closures of Boca Chica Beach and State Highway 4 from Cameron County to the new citySigned June 20, 2025. In force since Sept 1, 2025
Aug 2025Direct sale of park parcelsFour parcels, about 1.9 acres, which SpaceX wanted for employee housingSale authorized by the commission on Aug 21, 2025

The numbers tell the story. The reported 1,300 acres are about 30 times the 43 acres Texas Parks and Wildlife agreed to trade in 2024, and nearly twice the 715 refuge acres in the federal swap that cleared court in September. The earlier deals involved small, scattered parcels or trades for land elsewhere. This one, as reported, would take the whole park.

Why now: 25 launches a year vs 456

The timing fits SpaceX's plans. On October 14 the FAA published a notice opening an environmental review of SpaceX's request to fly Starship from Starbase up to 456 times a year. The current approval is up to 25. The FAA lays out two phases:

  • Phase 1: up to 100 launches and 200 landings a year, on any day and at any time.
  • Phase 2: up to 456 launches and 912 landings a year, with launches limited to Monday through Friday.

Nothing has been approved yet. Public comments are open until November 30 under docket FAA-2026-12808. The FAA will hold in-person meetings in Brownsville on November 17 and Port Isabel on November 19, and a virtual one on November 18.

Do the math and the land question is obvious. Going from 25 launches to 456 is more than 18 times the activity, and the plan counts up to 912 landings a year at the site, 456 for each stage. That's at a site Texas Monthly describes as surrounded by the state park. More flights would likely mean more beach and highway closures, more room for pads and support facilities, and bigger safety buffers. A neighbor that is public parkland makes every one of those harder. Owning that land would make them easier.

A sale would still need a public process

Discussing a land deal behind closed doors is allowed in Texas. The Texas Open Meetings Act lets public bodies deliberate the "purchase, exchange, lease, or value of real property" in a closed meeting when doing it in public would hurt their negotiating position. But under the same law, any final vote has to happen in an open meeting.

There's also a recent example of what a park sale involves. When the commission approved the 1.9-acre sale to SpaceX in August 2025, its resolution cited Chapter 26 of the Parks and Wildlife Code. That meant public notices in the Brownsville Herald, the Valley Morning Star and the Corpus Christi Caller-Times, a public hearing, and a formal finding that there was "no feasible and prudent alternative" to the use of the land. Selling an entire coastal park would face that same test, with a lot more people likely to turn up to the hearing. Environment America has already posted a petition on its Texas site asking the commission to reject any sale.

My read

From SpaceX's side, this makes sense. Starbase is where Starship is built, tested and launched. The company is asking the FAA for 18 times today's launch cap, and the land around the pad is the hard limit. A company that has spent three years pursuing parcels, swaps and closure powers will naturally want the whole park instead of fighting over it piece by piece.

From the public's side, this is different from the earlier deals. The 2024 swap offered Texans about ten times more land than they gave up. The 2025 parcels were tiny lots inside a subdivision. A full sale would mean the state handing over an entire coastal park, one Texas Monthly calls beloved, rather than trading a few scattered lots for something bigger. And putting it on a closed-door agenda two days after an election is the part that will get the most criticism, whatever the price. If Texas does reconsider it, the clearest path is the open one: publish the terms, show what the public gets in return, and hold a hearing under Chapter 26 as it did last year.

What to watch next

  • The final November agenda. Texas Parks and Wildlife posts agendas before each meeting. Watch whether any Cameron County land item appears, in open or closed session.
  • A response from SpaceX or Musk. We found none on X as of Saturday morning.
  • A swap instead of a sale. Two of the earlier Boca Chica deals were trades for land elsewhere. Any revived plan may look the same.
  • The FAA's 456-launch review. Scoping meetings run November 17–19, and comments close November 30.
  • The refuge lawsuit. The judge only refused to halt the federal swap. The conservation groups say the ruling "is not the final word."
