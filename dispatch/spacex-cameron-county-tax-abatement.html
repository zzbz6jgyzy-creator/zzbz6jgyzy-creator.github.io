SpaceX · Starbase · 19 Nov 2026

Cameron County says no to SpaceX's 10-year tax break for its $24 billion Starbase compute plant. SpaceX warns of a "revenue cliff"

Cameron County commissioners voted 3–2 on Tuesday not to hold a public hearing on SpaceX's request for a 10-year, 100% property-tax abatement on a gas power plant and supercomputer campus at Starbase. SpaceX answered on X that the county has now set itself up for swinging tax rates as the computers lose value. The vote doesn't stop construction, and the City of Starbase still has its own abatement hearing on 9 Dec.

What the county decided

On Tuesday, 17 Nov, the Commissioners Court voted against setting a public hearing on the abatement, ValleyCentral and The Brownsville Herald reported. The hearing would have been at the regular meeting on 29 Dec.

For a hearingAgainst
County Judge Eddie Treviño Jr.
Pct. 2 Commissioner Joey Lopez		Pct. 1 Commissioner Sofia Benavidez
Pct. 3 Commissioner David Garza
Pct. 4 Commissioner Gus Ruiz

Lopez told the Herald the vote was only about holding a hearing so the public could see the numbers, and that with this court the abatement is "dead in the water." Residents and groups including the South Texas Environmental Justice Network, Valley Interfaith and Surfrider Foundation spent most of the first hour asking commissioners to reject it.

What SpaceX asked for

From the application, as reported by the Herald (filed 15 Oct, revised 9 Nov):

  • A 10-year, 100% abatement, with fixed payments in lieu of taxes (PILOTs) still to be negotiated. No PILOT amount was made public.
  • About $24 billion of investment in "a natural gas-fired power plant and a co-located super compute facility" on about 444 acres, with $13.8 billion of taxable value in year one.
  • Construction from Q4 2026 to Q2 2027, and up to 5,500 construction workers at peak.
  • Behind-the-meter power, so the load stays off the ERCOT grid, and closed-loop cooling.

The application also said that without the abatement SpaceX would "redirect current and/or future investment" to compute sites outside Cameron County. That's a stated option in a filing. SpaceX hasn't said it's moving anything.

SpaceX's answer

At 6:14 p.m. ET (3:14 p.m. PT) on Wednesday, SpaceX posted its case:

The full statement, which ValleyCentral printed, says the abatement would have given the county "substantial, predictable new revenue" through PILOTs and ends: "Property owners could now face greater year-to-year fluctuations in tax rates as the equipment depreciates." SpaceX says it still wants talks with local governments. It also says it has put more than $15 billion into Starbase already, that the project has created more than 3,000 construction jobs so far, and that it will have more than 500 permanent jobs when completed.

The math behind the "cliff"

Texas taxing units work around a no-new-revenue rate, the rate that would bring in the same money from the same properties as last year. When existing values rise, that rate falls. When a big asset loses value fast, it rises. Servers lose value much faster than land or buildings, so a county that leans on a compute campus would see its base shrink every year unless rates go up.

For scale, here are the county's own numbers. Cameron County kept its rate at $0.424393 per $100 of value for 2026–27 and expects about $11.5 million more property-tax revenue, an 8.36% increase.

LineAmount
Year-one taxable value in SpaceX's application$13.8B
County tax on that at today's rate (my math)≈ $58.6M a year
County property-tax revenue this year, implied by +$11.5M = +8.36% (my math)≈ $149M
SpaceX plant vs that≈ 39%

County tax only. School district, city and other units levy their own rates. Year-one value only, before depreciation. The 2026–27 rate and revenue growth are from the county's 3 Sep budget release.

Both sides have a point in that table. One campus could be worth close to 40% of the county's current property-tax take in its first year, which is a lot to lose if the hardware is written down quickly. It's also a lot to give up for 10 years in exchange for a PILOT nobody has put a number on. The county's 2026–27 budget release notes its rate is already below the no-new-revenue rate of $0.545920.

What's still open

The county was only one of the taxing units involved. The City of Starbase notice for its own abatement and economic development agreement hearing, set for Wednesday, 9 Dec at 7 p.m. CT (8 p.m. ET / 5 p.m. PT), was still on the city's homepage today. As I wrote on 10 Nov, two of Starbase's three commissioners work for SpaceX.

My read

SpaceX is right that depreciating servers are hard to tax, and the county is right to balk at a 10-year break when the PILOT has no number attached. The plant keeps going either way, since this vote only stopped a hearing. The thing to watch is whether SpaceX puts a PILOT figure in public before 9 Dec. Should a poor county give a $24 billion campus a 10-year break for predictable payments, or tax it in full and take the swings? Tell me below.
