SpaceX · AI · 15 Nov 2026

Musk says his Nvidia deals ran on Jensen Huang's word. Here's how much of SpaceX's AI now rests on Nvidia

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's CEO on 11 Nov. On Saturday, Elon Musk said many of the supply deals Huang struck were "a handshake with Jensen, not a written contract," and that "at least some of those critical deals will be lost." He added that his own requests to Huang worked the same way, with purchase orders following later. Nvidia's board says the opposite: customer commitments will "continue without interruption." SpaceX has a lot riding on which of them is right.

The editor holds Tesla and SpaceX shares. Supercharged Daily is independent and not affiliated with either company or with Nvidia. Nothing here is financial advice.

What happened at Nvidia

Nvidia's Form 8-K says the board accepted Huang's resignation as president, CEO and director on 11 Nov 2026, effective immediately. The company announced it on 12 Nov. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO while the board searches, and longtime director Harvey C. Jones is Nvidia's first-ever board chair. The AP reports that he left after reports of extramarital affairs, one of which the New York Times said involved a woman U.S. intelligence suspects of spying for China. Beijing denies that.

Nvidia's release includes this line from Jones: "we expect the company's operations, customer commitments and product roadmap to continue without interruption."

What Musk said

Musk has backed Huang all week. On Friday, 13 Nov, at 3:51 p.m. ET (12:51 p.m. PT), he wrote that "a petition is underway to reinstate Jensen and I'm signing it too." On Saturday morning he quoted a clip of SemiAnalysis founder Dylan Patel, who says Huang personally talked small suppliers into selling their output to Nvidia.

Forty-three minutes later he made it about himself:

That's the head of one of Nvidia's best-known AI customers saying publicly that at least some of his compute deliveries were agreed by word first and put on paper afterwards. He didn't say which deals or how much hardware, and SpaceX hasn't filed anything about it.

Compiled from the linked SpaceX filings and posts, as of 15 Nov 2026. The Memphis counts are Musk's figures, not audited numbers.

Adding up Musk's own numbers gives about 860,000 GB300s across the four Memphis buildings (my math: three buildings at 220,000 plus about 200,000). Roughly 420,000 of them, close to half, sit in Gun Range and Spam, the two buildings he says come online in late November and December. He hasn't said whether those chips are already on site.

The Google contract runs the other way: it's money coming in for Nvidia hardware that SpaceX rents out. At $920 million a month it comes to about $11.04 billion a year (my math).

Where a lost handshake would hurt

My read, not anything SpaceX or Nvidia has said: the part most exposed to Musk's worry is anything not yet delivered that was agreed informally. That most likely means the rest of the GB300s for Gun Range and Spam, plus any "accelerated" components he asked Huang for. The Vera Rubin hardware looks safer, because a signed $40 billion financing with Apollo and big Wall Street backers sits behind it. The 8-K, though, covers the money. It doesn't describe the supply agreement with Nvidia, and neither has been published.

Musk also isn't a neutral witness. He's publicly campaigning to get Huang reinstated, and saying deals will be lost without him helps that case. Nvidia's board has every reason to say the opposite. Neither side has given details.

SpaceX has a long-term hedge in Terafab, the chip fab it's planning with Tesla, and it describes the Starmind design as chip-vendor agnostic. Neither helps in the next six weeks.

Update, 18 Nov 2026: Nvidia's first results without Huang are out. The release says Vera Rubin NVL72 racks are "coming online soon at SpaceXSI." I went through the figures and the 2027 lease dates in a new piece.

What to watch

  • 17 Nov, now past: Nvidia reported Q3 results. The release named SpaceXSI in the Vera Rubin sentence; see the follow-up.
  • Wednesday, 18 Nov: Kress told employees the company will hold a staff meeting, Reuters reports.
  • End of November: whether Gun Range comes online on Musk's schedule.
  • Before 31 Dec: the closing filing for the Apollo lease.

Whose version do you believe, Musk's handshake warning or Nvidia's "without interruption"?

— Alex R
