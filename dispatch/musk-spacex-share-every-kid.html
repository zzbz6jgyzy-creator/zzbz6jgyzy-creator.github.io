Musk says he'll give a SpaceX share to every kid in America. Here's what that's worth, and how it could actually work

At 3:52am ET, Elon Musk posted a one-line pledge: next year, every kid in America gets one of his SpaceX shares, and "this will be the first of many." With about 72 million Americans under 18 and SpaceX closing Friday at $146.03, that's roughly $10.5 billion, which would make it the biggest gift of its kind so far. He gave no details on how it would be done, but there's already a government system built for exactly this kind of gift, and one SpaceX executive has already pledged shares through it.

It spread fast. Within about 90 minutes it had passed 2.9 million views and 85,000 likes. Musk didn't say what counts as a "kid," when in 2027 the shares would arrive, or what "first of many" means, and nothing further had been posted in the thread when this was published.

The math

The Census Bureau count cited by CNBC is 72 million children under 18 as of July 2025. One share each at Friday's closing price of $146.03 comes to about $10.5 billion. The real figure will depend on the share price on the day, and on how many children are eligible by then.

For Musk, it's a small slice. According to an August SEC filing cited by CNBC, he directly owns about 4.8 billion SpaceX shares, or 6.4 billion including unvested awards and options. Seventy-two million shares is about 1.5% of the 4.8 billion.

"First of many" is where it gets interesting. If, and this is purely my own illustration since Musk gave no schedule, he repeated the gift every year for a decade, that would be about 720 million shares, roughly 15% of his directly owned stake at today's count. A child born in 2027 who got one share a year until turning 18 would end up with 18 shares, plus whatever they grow into.

Two things stand out. Musk's gift would be about 1.7 times the Dell pledge, which Axios called the previous record. And it's the only one in the table with no income or age test, at least as written. The Dell and Shotwell gifts were deliberately aimed at lower-income areas, while Musk's covers everyone.

Per child, though, it's smaller than the other big gifts: about $146 against Dell's $250 and the Treasury's $1,000. The difference is that Musk's gift is stock, not cash. If SpaceX grows, so does every child's share. If it falls, so does the gift.

The pipe already exists

Musk didn't mention Trump Accounts, the tax-advantaged investment accounts for American children that launched on July 4. But the program has already been set up for gifts like this one:

  • Stock gifts are allowed. On July 2, the Treasury said it would accept large philanthropic gifts of publicly traded stock and deliver the shares into children's accounts according to the donor's instructions.
  • Every child can be reached. Under Treasury's September 30 rules, the government was to open an account automatically, on or about October 1, 2026, for every child who meets the age and Social Security number requirements and doesn't already have one, with more sign-ups added regularly after that. That solves the hardest part of Musk's pledge, which is finding 72 million children.
  • There's a SpaceX precedent, on paper. Shotwell pledged one SpaceX share each to more than 2 million Trump Accounts in July. That was before the rules were final, and under them stock gifts now have to come through a charity and win Treasury approval, Fortune reported on 9 October. We haven't seen a report that her shares have been delivered yet.

Musk has been linked to the idea before. In early July, President Trump told CNBC he thought Musk would donate company stock to the program. That didn't happen at the time, and Monday's post still doesn't say which route he'll take.

The open questions

  • Who counts as a "kid"? Under 18 is the obvious reading, but he didn't say. Does a baby born in 2027 count? What about a 17-year-old who turns 18 before the gift arrives?
  • Can his shares be used? Treasury's rules accept only stock with "no pre-existing transfer restrictions," and give restricted securities as the example. According to the lock-up schedule in SpaceX's prospectus, Musk's own shares stay locked up until June 12, 2027. Gifts are often allowed under lock-up agreements, but how this would work for Musk's shares hasn't been spelled out. It may affect when "next year" actually happens.
  • Can kids sell? If the shares go through Trump Accounts, the rules say gifted stock generally can't be sold for five years, or until the account's growth period ends around age 18 if that comes first. For a 16-year-old, that's about two years. For a toddler, it's the full five years, after which they can be sold inside the account.
  • What does "first of many" mean? Every year? Every milestone? Musk hasn't said.

The timing

The post lands at a busy moment for SpaceX stock. The company reports third-quarter results on Tuesday, and on Thursday up to 1.3 billion early-investor and employee shares become free to sell, the largest single release in its lock-up schedule. It also came a day after Anthropic said it would put 5% of its shares into a new public benefit fund, a point CNBC noted too.

My read

The dollar figure gets the headlines, but the bigger idea is ownership. Seventy-two million children holding even one share each would make SpaceX one of the most widely held stocks in the country overnight, and every one of those kids would have a reason to follow its launches. Musk has spent years saying he wants people to feel part of the mission to Mars. Handing them a piece of it is the most literal way to do that.

The plumbing is mostly in place already: the Treasury accepts stock, automatic accounts reach almost every child, and Shotwell's pledge has already tested the idea with SpaceX shares. What's missing are the details only Musk can give: who qualifies, when, and through which route. Tuesday's earnings call would be a natural place to ask.

What to watch

  • Any follow-up from Musk or SpaceX on eligibility and timing.
  • Whether the Treasury or the Trump Accounts team confirms the gift is going through their system.
  • Whether it comes up on Tuesday's SpaceX earnings call, Tue 4:30pm ET.
