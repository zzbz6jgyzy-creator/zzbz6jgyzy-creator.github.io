Barclays says a Tesla–SpaceX merger is becoming “market consensus”, and could be announced before Tesla’s AGM

The bank sees an all-stock deal with SpaceX buying Tesla as the most likely shape. Nothing has been announced: this is analyst speculation, and the shareholder vote would be the hardest part.

From @ALJR86Markets desk · Speculation

Important: no merger has been proposed or confirmed by Tesla or SpaceX. Everything below is analyst speculation, and none of it is financial advice.

Combining Elon Musk’s two biggest companies has moved from fan theory to a Wall Street base case. In a note to clients on Wednesday, Barclays analysts led by Dan Levy wrote that a Tesla–SpaceX merger “has increasingly become the consensus expectation” (Bloomberg, via The Business Times), and said an announcement could come before Tesla’s 2026 annual meeting.

The timing

Barclays said corporate rules require Tesla to hold its annual meeting by 6 December. The bank thinks an announcement could still slip to late October or early November, but warned that the later it comes, the harder it becomes to get a vote done at that meeting (Yahoo Finance; The Asia Business Daily). If the deal is announced separately, Barclays still expects news within six to 12 months.

The likely shape

Barclays thinks the most likely structure is SpaceX buying Tesla in an all-stock deal, with Tesla shareholders getting SpaceX shares. At market values of roughly $2.1 trillion for SpaceX and $1.5 trillion for Tesla, the bank estimates about 0.7 SpaceX shares per Tesla share, before any premium for Tesla holders (Yahoo Finance).

Control is the key point. Musk owns about 48% of SpaceX and around 82% of its voting power through dual-class shares. Barclays calculates that if SpaceX acquired Tesla, he would own about 37% of the combined company and keep roughly 56% of the votes. Musk has long said he wants about 25% of Tesla to feel comfortable pushing AI and robotics. SpaceX’s share structure is the reason Barclays sees it as the buyer.

Why it is on the table

The two companies already overlap. They share a CEO and many investors. SpaceX has bought more than $100 million of Cybertrucks. xAI, now part of SpaceX, is a major Megapack customer. The two are also working on Terafab, a joint chip project for vehicles, Optimus and orbital AI hardware (The Asia Business Daily). Barclays said the main value would come from tying together the two companies’ AI work, not cost savings.

The hard part: the vote

Shareholders at both companies would probably have to approve a deal. Barclays pointed out that around 11% of the total votes in Tesla’s 2025 annual meeting were broker non-votes, which in practice count as votes against, and expects Tesla to push hard for turnout. Antitrust review would also apply.

The bigger numbers

The merger talk lands on top of some huge forecasts. Billionaire investor Ron Baron has said he thinks SpaceX could be worth $20 trillion to $50 trillion in 10 years. Responding to a post about that, Musk wrote: “Unless there is a major world war, SpaceX probably gets to >$100T in <10 years.” On Wall Street, Raymond James analyst Brian Gesuale reiterated a Strong Buy rating and an $800 price target on $SPCX this week, the highest on the Street. He cited SpaceX’s FAA filing to expand Starship launches from Texas.

These are forecasts and opinions. Musk’s long-range targets have a history of arriving late, and an analyst’s target is not a promise.

Speculation: what it could mean for holders

If Barclays is right, $TSLA holders would end up owning a slice of SpaceX, including Starlink, launch and xAI, while $SPCX holders would take on Tesla’s cars, energy, robotaxi and Optimus businesses. The real unknowns are the premium, the vote and the regulators. Until either company says something, every number here is a scenario.

Sources: Barclays note of 14 Oct 2026 as reported by Yahoo Finance, The Asia Business Daily and The Business Times/Bloomberg. Raymond James via Investing.com and StreetInsider.

It’s not a deal until two boards and a lot of shareholders say yes. But for the first time, a big bank is treating it as the base case.

Not financial advice. I may hold $TSLA and/or $SPCX.
