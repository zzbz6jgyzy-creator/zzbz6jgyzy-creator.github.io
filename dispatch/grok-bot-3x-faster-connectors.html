Grok · Grok Bot · 17 Oct 2026

Grok Bot says it's 3x faster and now plugs into Robinhood, Coinbase and your X DMs. Here's what actually changed

On Friday afternoon, the official Grok Bot account posted a nine-part update. It claims computer tasks now finish three times sooner on average, and it lists a new look, sharper voice calls, access to X Chat, and new connectors for Robinhood, Coinbase, eToro and others. Elon Musk followed up with "Grok @Bot got much faster." Some of these are genuinely new. Some were already live, and this is the first time they've been announced together. The speed figure is the company's own claim. The money connectors deserve a closer look.

Updated 27 Oct 2026

The @bot thread went up at Fri 3:55pm ET, opening with "We've made some improvements to Grok Bot's quality, polish, and speed." About three and a half hours later, at Fri 7:35pm ET, Musk quote-posted the speed claim with "Grok @Bot got much faster." By early Saturday that post had more than 900,000 views, by X's count. A minute and a half before it, he had posted a separate tip: "Also, ask Grok @Bot to connect to your 𝕏 DMs. Best way to use it."

The headline number: "3x sooner"

The exact wording from @bot is: "Grok Bot is much faster. Computer tasks finish 3x sooner on average, and replies to everything else arrive in less than half the time." Computer tasks are the jobs a Bot does on its own cloud computer, like browsing a website, filling a form or running a command.

Taken at face value, a browser job that used to take nine minutes would now take about three, and a chat reply that took ten seconds would take under five. That's simple arithmetic on the company's claimed averages, not a test of mine. Your own jobs will vary.

Two caveats. First, it's the company's own figure, with no published benchmark or method that I could find. Second, it isn't on the official Grok Bot changelog yet. On Friday night the newest entry there was still version 0.70.2, dated 11 Oct. Speed has been creeping up for weeks, though. The 7 Oct release already said Bots "work faster on their computer," with screenshots coming back quicker and new windows skipping "a five-second wait," and it bumped the Bot's screen to 1920 × 1200 from 1280 × 800. On 29 Sep, a release made Bots move through websites faster, with shorter waits after each click.

The speed claim gets the headline. The brokerage connectors are the change that deserves the most care.

Everything in Friday's update, sorted

Not every item in the thread is brand new. Here's my sorting, checked against the changelog and earlier posts:

  • Speed (new claim): computer tasks 3x sooner on average, other replies in under half the time, according to @bot.
  • New look (new): redesigned chat cards with "a cleaner layout," plus a new typeface and themes.
  • Voice calls (improved): "faster, more reliable, and better at remembering past conversations."
  • X Chat access (new this week): connect X, then ask your Bot to work across your messages and message requests. Vann says he'd had his Bot "reviewing new message requests all week, approving them" before it launched.
  • New connectors (new): Robinhood, Coinbase, Trello, PostHog, beehiiv, eToro "and more." Teams can now submit their own connectors to the Grok Bot marketplace for review.
  • Notifications (new): alerts now go "only to the device you're using."
  • Email plus aliases (announced Wednesday): a routine can run only on mail sent to one alias. SpaceXSI's Sam Sokolin announced this at Wed 6:21pm ET.
  • Tag @bot on X (already live): mention @bot in a post and your Bot picks up the job. It was already being used on Thursday. One widely shared post, which @bot quoted in the thread, asked it to preorder the new Roadster.

The money connectors: what to know before you link an account

Robinhood, Coinbase and eToro are a different category from Trello or PostHog. They hold real money. Friday's post doesn't say what a Bot can actually do through them, whether that's reading balances and positions or also placing orders.

Robinhood's own help pages, which list Grok among the AI platforms that can connect to its trading connection, are more specific. An agent connected that way gets read access to all your Robinhood accounts, including account numbers, positions, balances and order history. It can place trades, but only in a separate "MCP account" you open for the agent, not your main account. And Robinhood says trade approvals are turned off by default for those external-agent accounts, so the agent "can place eligible trades for you without your manual confirmation of each order." Neither SpaceXSI nor Robinhood has said whether Grok Bot's new connector works exactly the same way, so check before you sign in.

What the Grok Bot changelog does already promise is a set of safety rails that apply to these connectors:

  • Approval before anything permanent. Since 11 Oct, "before an action it can't undo, such as sending, posting, buying, or deleting, a Bot checks your own messages for approval."
  • Financial data stays put. Also since 11 Oct: "After a bank or brokerage connector returns data, the Bot asks before sharing it anywhere else."
  • Who pays for mistakes. Musk has said SpaceX will "make you whole" if a Grok Bot shopping errand goes wrong, but the Grok Bot Terms put more of the risk on the user. We broke down that gap earlier this week. Read that before you let a Bot near a brokerage account. Robinhood's disclosures say the same thing from its side: you "assume all risk for trades executed by AI agents."

My advice: if you connect Robinhood, turn its trade approvals on, keep only what you're willing to risk in the agent's account, and keep Grok Bot's own approval prompts switched on.

Why X Chat access matters

Musk singled this one out himself, calling it the "best way to use it." It makes sense. X is Musk's own platform, and DMs plus message requests are where a busy account's inbox piles up. A Bot that can triage message requests, as in Vann's demo, ties X more tightly to Grok Bot than any outside tool is likely to.

It also means giving an AI agent access to your private messages, so it's worth knowing what you're switching on. The thread says it starts with connecting X. As with any connector, check what access you're granting when you sign in.

What to watch next

  • The changelog entry. A new version on x.ai/changelog/bot should spell out exactly what each new connector can do.
  • Trading permissions. Whether the Coinbase and eToro connectors are read-only or can place trades, and whether Grok Bot's Robinhood connector matches Robinhood's own setup.
  • Independent speed tests. Whether users timing the same tasks before and after see anything close to 3x.
  • Third-party connectors. With submissions open, how fast the marketplace grows, and how strict SpaceXSI's review turns out to be.
