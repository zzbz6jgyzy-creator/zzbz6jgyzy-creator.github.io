Tesla · Robotaxi · 27 Nov 2026

Tesla's Dallas Robotaxi zone grows to about 150 square miles, three times Waymo's. Its eight-city map is up 65% this month

Tesla's Robotaxi account posted a new Dallas map on Wednesday, 25 Nov. The service area now crosses I-635 into Farmers Branch and runs up toward Carrollton, Addison and Richardson. Tesla didn't give a size. Sawyer Merritt measured about 150 square miles, up 83% from 82. Waymo's Dallas area was about 50 square miles at its last published count. Add up Tesla's eight cities on Merritt's figures and you get 1,374 square miles, against 831 on 2 Nov.

What the new map covers

Tesla's map, labelled "Dallas, TX," keeps the old zone around downtown Dallas and University Park and adds a large block to the north. The new blue line crosses I-635 (the LBJ Freeway) and takes in Farmers Branch. Its top edge runs just below the Addison label. On the west it stops at Carrollton, and on the east it follows the edge of Richardson. A thin strip was also added along the western side, toward Irving. The post's text is short: "Just grew wider in Dallas. More cars, less $, Happy Thanksgiving!"

Tesla doesn't publish square mileage, fares or fleet counts per city, and this post gives none of them. Everything below that puts a number on the area comes from people measuring Tesla's maps, mainly Merritt, so treat the figures as close estimates.

How big it is

Six minutes before Tesla's post went up, Sawyer Merritt wrote that the Dallas area had grown 83% to 150 square miles, from 82. About a minute after it, he posted an updated list for all eight Robotaxi cities. When Fort Worth launched on 2 Nov, I used his earlier list in my Fort Worth piece. Putting the two side by side shows how much has changed in 23 days:

Figures: Sawyer Merritt's measurements of Tesla's official maps (2 Nov, 25 Nov). He gave Dallas as 81 on 2 Nov and 82 as the "before" figure on 25 Nov, a rounding difference. Change and totals are my arithmetic.

Three things stand out to me. Dallas is now slightly bigger than Fort Worth (150 against 143), which wasn't true when Fort Worth opened with almost twice Dallas's area. The five Texas cities add up to 1,265 square miles, 92% of the total, so Florida is still a small part of the map at 109. And there have been five launches or expansions in November, counting Fort Worth's opening on 2 Nov.

Dallas against Waymo

Waymo opened Dallas to invited riders on 24 Feb and to everyone on 4 Aug, after serving "nearly 150,000 riders" from its interest list. Its area is about 50 square miles of central Dallas, bounded by Northwest Highway to the north and reaching Bishop Arts and parts of South Dallas to the south, as Dallas Innovates reported. That's the last published figure I can find. On Merritt's measurement, Tesla's Dallas zone is now about three times that.

Area isn't the whole comparison. Waymo has been running in Dallas for nine months and has published a rider count; Tesla hasn't said how many cars serve Dallas. Waymo said in August that it was testing at the Love Field terminals and would soon start driverless testing on Dallas freeways. In November it paused freeway rides nationwide after California's DMV banned its cars from state freeways, AP reported, so its Dallas trips stay on surface streets for now. Tesla's new map has stretches of I-635 and the Dallas North Tollway inside it, but Tesla hasn't said whether Dallas rides use them.

Dallas and Fort Worth still don't connect

The two zones add up to 293 square miles across the metro, but they're separate. On Tesla's maps the Dallas zone's western edge reaches Irving, and the Fort Worth map stops around Haltom City and Forest Hill on its east side. Arlington, which sits between the two, isn't in either. A ride from downtown Dallas to downtown Fort Worth isn't possible on these maps.

What's confirmed and what isn't

  • Confirmed by Tesla: the Dallas area was expanded on 25 Nov, with the official old and new map, and the post says "More cars, less $."
  • Measured by trackers, not Tesla: 150 square miles, the 83% increase and the eight-city total of 1,374. Merritt describes all eight areas as running fully autonomous and unsupervised; Tesla's post doesn't address that.
  • Not published: Dallas fares, how many cars serve Dallas and whether airport or freeway trips are included. "Less $" has no number attached, and I haven't seen a fare table to check it against.
  • Background on Dallas: Cybercab rides began there on 19 Nov, and Tesla says Robotaxi runs 24/7 in every city it serves.

My read

Six days after Cybercabs arrived in Dallas, Tesla nearly doubled the area they can drive in. A 65% jump in total area in 23 days is fast, and 82% of it came from two cities, Austin and San Antonio. I'd like to see Tesla publish its own area and fleet numbers so we don't have to measure maps. The next thing I'm watching is whether the Dallas and Fort Worth zones join up across Arlington. If you ride in Dallas, are fares actually lower? Tell me below.

— Alex R
