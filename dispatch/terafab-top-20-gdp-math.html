Tesla · SpaceX · Terafab · 21 Oct 2026

Musk says Terafab's output will rank in the top 20 of world GDP. I checked the math

To rank in the top 20 of world GDP, Terafab would have to turn out about $1.14 trillion of chips a year. That's roughly 69% of the entire world chip market this year, and about six times what TSMC, the world's biggest chipmaker, is bringing in right now. Elon Musk says it will happen "in a single building." Could one fab really do that, and what would have to be true?

Updated 27 Oct 2026

What Musk said

On Tuesday, October 20, at 1:02pm ET (10:02am PT) (shown in your time zone), Musk posted on X: "The value of Terafab’s output will place it in the top 20 of country GDP in a single building". By Wednesday morning it had been viewed about 2.4 million times. He was quote-posting another account's summary of a report from Korean trade outlet The Elec about Terafab's supplier talks.

Later that day, on SpaceX's third-quarter earnings call, he gave the project dates. Terafab aims to have a first working test chip, built with SRAM circuits (a common way to tune a new production line), out of its research fab in Austin in "approximately May or June of next year." He said volume production is aimed at around Q3 2028, "at least in the set of possible outcomes for Q3 or Q4 of ’28," and called them "very aggressive dates" with "big error bars" (Reuters; TrendForce). Asked whether SpaceX had ordered ASML's EUV machines, he didn't answer directly, but said "we’re very appreciative of ASML and all of our other suppliers in accelerating equipment deliveries" (Reuters).

— Elon Musk (@elonmusk) October 20, 2026

What's done, and what's only planned

  • Done (announced by the state): On August 6, Texas Governor Greg Abbott announced that SpaceX will build Terafab in Grimes County. He put the first phase at more than $16.8 billion and 3,000 jobs, in a 100-million-square-foot facility. SpaceX got a $30 million Texas Enterprise Fund grant, which is performance-based (KBTX).
  • Done (on file): The Texas Comptroller's JETI tax-incentive list shows eight agreements for "TeraFab AI, LLC". They cover four phases across two school districts, Anderson-Shiro and Iola, and add up to $120 billion of proposed investment. Phase 1 is $16 billion of that ($10 billion plus $6 billion).
  • Reported, not confirmed by the companies: Tesla and SpaceX staff visited the booths of about 10 Korean suppliers at SEMICON West in San Francisco on October 13, and more than half have signed NDAs, The Elec says. HPSP has agreed to supply one high-pressure hydrogen annealing tool for the research line, and ISC is exchanging samples. Most of the rest is still at the talks stage.
  • Targets (Musk's own, with "big error bars"): a test chip around May or June 2027, volume production around Q3 2028, and in the long run a terawatt a year of computing power for AI and robotics (Reuters). The Elec reports the goal of 1 million wafers a month within 10 years.
  • Outside forecasts: SemiAnalysis expects the first phase to start running in late 2029 and reach volume production by mid-2030, according to Bits&Chips. Industry observers told The Elec the first 100,000-wafer-a-month facility should be finished before 2030.

Analysis: what "top 20 GDP" means in dollars

In the IMF's October 2026 World Economic Outlook, the 20th-largest economy in 2026 is Switzerland at about $1.136 trillion, just ahead of Poland at $1.133 trillion (table). So taken literally, Musk's claim means Terafab turning out more than about $1.14 trillion a year. Here's how that compares with today's chip industry:

BenchmarkFigure$1.14T vs it
Whole world chip market, 2026 (WSTS, Aug 2026)$1.655 trillionAbout 69% of it
World chip market, 2027 (same WSTS release)About $2.1 trillionAbout 54% of it
TSMC, Q3 2026 revenue (TSMC)$46.72 billion a quarter, about $187 billion a year at that paceAbout 6 times it
Terafab phase-1 investment (Texas Governor)More than $16.8 billionAbout 68 times the phase-1 spend, every year

My calculations from the linked figures. The WSTS 2026 number is its updated full-year estimate, not a final count. TSMC's yearly figure is simply Q3 multiplied by four, not a forecast.

Now look at it per wafer. The Elec says the long-term goal is 1 million wafers a month within 10 years. TSMC's 2025 annual report puts its total capacity at more than 17 million 12-inch-equivalent wafers a year. That's about 1.4 million a month across all its fabs worldwide, so Terafab's goal is about 70% of TSMC's whole footprint, in one project.

At 12 million wafers a year, $1.14 trillion works out to about $95,000 of output per wafer. At the first phase's reported 100,000 wafers a month, it would take about $950,000 per wafer, far above anything a fab earns per wafer today. So the "top 20" line only adds up at full build-out. It also depends on counting the value of finished parts: the governor's office says Terafab will combine logic, memory and advanced packaging under one roof (KBTX), rather than selling bare wafers.

One more caveat: GDP counts the value added inside a country, while Musk is talking about the "value of output." Those aren't the same thing. A fab's output value includes the materials and tools it buys in, so the comparison is loose by design.

Why it matters for Tesla and SpaceX holders

Terafab is one reason chips keep coming up in the merger talk. On the same call, Musk said special committees at both companies are looking at a combination, but no terms are agreed (see my SpaceX Q3 results piece). The Elec notes that Tesla, SpaceX and xAI said in March they'd pursue Terafab together, but they haven't disclosed a separate entity or ownership structure. The state's announcement names SpaceX, and the tax filings name an LLC called TeraFab AI. Who pays for the later phases, and who books the revenue, is the question to watch.

Tesla reports its own Q3 results after the US market closes today, with the call at 5:30pm ET (2:30pm PT) (shown in your time zone) on Tesla's investor site. Analysts may well ask how much of Terafab's cost and output is Tesla's.

My read

This is my opinion. The state announcement and the tax filings are real, and the supplier talks The Elec describes are moving fast for a chip project. The $1.14 trillion is a target for the end of a decade-long build. It means one site making more than half of what WSTS expects the whole industry to sell in 2027, and about six times TSMC's current pace. I'd treat "top 20 GDP" as Musk's statement of ambition, not a forecast. The milestones to watch are the Austin test chip next May or June, the first EUV tool arriving, and whether phase 1 runs on time. If those land near Musk's dates, the big number gets harder to laugh off.
