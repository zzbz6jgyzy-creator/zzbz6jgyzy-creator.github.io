9,000 meters became 60. Stargaze saw it.

From @ALJR86 Launch footage desk

Five hours before a pass in late 2025, a Starlink satellite was set to miss another company’s satellite by about 9,000 meters. Then the other satellite fired its engine without telling anyone. The predicted miss shrank to about 60 meters.

That is the whole story in two numbers. Everything else is why those numbers exist.

Conjunctions are supposed to be boring. You publish an ephemeris, the other operator publishes theirs, and both sides agree the gap is fat. 9 km is fat. 60 meters is a near-miss you only talk about if you have sensors of your own, because the public catalogue will still be showing the old plan.

Starlink’s own sensor network is called Stargaze. That is the sentence I wanted on the timeline: not “space is crowded,” not a vibe about debris. A named network watched a silent burn collapse a miss by two orders of magnitude.

Why I posted the clip

A lot of Starlink talk is constellation size — 11,000 now, tens of thousands later, a million if you count Starmind. Size is not the hard part once you can fly the buses. The hard part is the other objects that do not file a burn notice. If you only trust the shared catalogue, you are driving on someone else’s indicator.

This is also why I keep the SpaceX desk next to Tesla and Grok instead of treating them as three fandoms. The same stack — sensors, autonomy, a willingness to fly the ugly version first — shows up in FSD, in Starship heatshields, and in a satellite that has to dodge a stranger.

I am not a conjunction analyst. I am the fan account that puts the number in the first line and the footage under it. If you want the live version, it is still on X.
