AI hardware · 24 Nov 2026

Musk: AI5 goes to Optimus and cars stay on AI4. AI4.5 loses 8GB of planned RAM because memory got expensive

From Alex R AI hardware

In a run of replies on X on Sunday, Elon Musk said Tesla's next chip, AI5, is going to the Optimus robot and that "Cars will remain at AI4." He also said AI4 now costs Tesla twice as much because of memory and storage prices, and that the planned AI4.5 board was cut from 32GB to 24GB of RAM. These are posts from Tesla's CEO. Tesla hasn't published a hardware roadmap, prices or dates to go with them.

The editor holds Tesla and SpaceX shares and drives a Model 3. Supercharged Daily is independent and not affiliated with Tesla or SpaceX. Nothing here is financial advice.

What Musk said, in order

It started with Reality View, the new FSD v15 screen. At 6:06am ET (3:06am PT) on Sunday, 22 Nov, Musk explained that the visualizer runs on "half a TRIP out of three on SoC B," TRIP being the neural-network accelerator on Tesla's AI4 computer, and added: "AI5 is a gigantic improvement. Roughly 12X more compute."

An owner then asked when AI5 would be in new cars, saying he felt he should wait before upgrading from his HW3 car. Musk answered at 7:09am ET (4:09am PT):

Three minutes earlier he had written that "AI4 costs twice as much now due to memory and storage price hikes." In the afternoon he turned to the in-between board. At 2:26pm ET he said AI4.5 "is a relatively small performance improvement that is mostly due to needing to upgrade RAM to LPDDR5X," and at 5:31pm ET (2:31pm PT) he added: "RAM is super expensive, so we reduced AI4.5 from 32GB to 24GB. That will be fine. It's hard to use 32GB anyway, given memory bandwidth constraints."

How this lines up with the earnings calls

The Optimus-first plan isn't new. What Sunday added is the flat "cars will remain at AI4," the cost figure and the smaller AI4.5.

The quotes come from transcripts of the Q2 2026 and Q3 2026 calls. On the Q2 call Musk also thanked Micron for a memory allocation "on reasonable terms given the pretty insane pricing of memory these days," so Sunday's cost comment matches what he was already telling investors in July.

What it means if you drive a Tesla

If your car has HW4 (AI4), Musk's position is that its computer is enough for the driving Tesla is promising. That's a claim about the future: FSD is still supervised, and Tesla hasn't shown unsupervised driving on customer cars.

HW3 owners are the group with the open question. On the Q2 call Musk said it would make sense "at some point" to upgrade cars below HW4, probably to "the next generation" board, either the upgraded AI4 or AI5. Sunday's answer points to the AI4 family, which now means AI4.5. Tesla hasn't announced a retrofit program, a price or a date.

If you were holding off on a purchase for AI5, on Musk's word there's no AI5 car to wait for. He didn't give an end date for AI4 in cars, so this could change with a later chip, but nothing he said suggests AI5 itself is coming to vehicles.

The memory math

Musk didn't say how much RAM AI4 has today or what it costs, so I can't put a dollar figure on "twice as much." The two numbers he did give point the same way. Cutting AI4.5 from 32GB to 24GB is a 25% reduction in planned memory, made because of price. With AI4.5 described mostly as a memory upgrade, a smaller memory upgrade also means a smaller step up from AI4. That fits his own description of it as "relatively small." Every car Tesla builds carries one of these computers, so a cost change there reaches margins quickly.

My read

As a shareholder, I read this as Tesla choosing where its scarcest parts go. AI5 has an order of magnitude more compute, and a humanoid robot has far more joints to control than a car has, which is the reason Musk gave on the Q3 call. Putting AI5 there and keeping cars on a cheaper, proven board makes sense if AI4 really is enough to drive, and that is the bet: Tesla is now tying its car autonomy plans to the computer already in its cars, while the memory that goes into it gets more expensive. The FSD v15 notes, released the day before, are the first evidence of how much Tesla can still fit on that hardware: Musk says the new driving view runs on half of one accelerator. Tell me below: if you're on HW3, would you pay for an AI4.5 retrofit, or wait for Tesla to offer something?

— Alex R
