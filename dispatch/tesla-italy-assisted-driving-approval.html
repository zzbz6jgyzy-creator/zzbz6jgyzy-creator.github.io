Tesla · FSD in Europe · 20 Nov 2026

Italy approves Tesla Assisted Driving, the 12th EU country and the first big one. The EU math still doesn't add up

Italy's transport ministry has given Tesla's supervised driver-assist system, the product Americans know as FSD (Supervised), a provisional approval without waiting for Brussels. Tesla says the rollout "begins soon." Italy is the first of the EU's four most populous countries to say yes, and it nearly doubles the share of EU residents living where the system is allowed. That share is still well short of what an EU-wide vote needs.

What Italy approved

Matteo Salvini, Italy's infrastructure and transport minister, announced it on X at 6:26 a.m. ET (3:26 a.m. PT) on Friday. He wrote that the ministry, "dopo puntuale verifica tecnica di fattibilità" (after a careful technical feasibility check), had given the green light to the "omologazione provvisoria", the provisional approval, of Tesla Assisted Driving. He added that Italy was moving ahead of the EU recognition process, which he described as "attualmente in fase di definizione" (still being worked out).

Tesla Europe had already posted an hour earlier, at 5:21 a.m. ET (2:21 a.m. PT): "Mamma, butta la pasta / Now approved in Italy / Rollout begins soon," over a 31-second video of a car driving itself through Rome, past the Colosseum and down streets barely wider than the car.

Neither post gives a rollout date. Il Sole 24 Ore reports that Tesla told the Italian press the feature will arrive as a software update and cost €99 a month by subscription. That price isn't in Tesla's post or Salvini's, so treat it as reported until Tesla lists it.

Elon Musk reposted a post by Andrea Stroppa at 6:23 a.m. ET that called Italy "the first of the large European countries" to approve and thanked Rome mayor Roberto Gualtieri, Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni, Salvini and opposition leader Carlo Calenda (Stroppa's post, Musk's repost). Stroppa's post calls it "guida autonoma," autonomous driving. The approval itself is for a supervised system. Tesla's own Italian product page says the current features "richiedono la supervisione attiva del conducente e non rendono autonomo il veicolo" (they require active driver supervision and don't make the car autonomous). The driver stays responsible for the car.

Twelve countries, and what Italy adds

The Netherlands' RDW approved the system on 10 Apr 2026, and other EU countries have been recognising that approval one at a time since. Finland was the 11th on 11 Nov. Italy is the 12th, which matches the count Il Sole 24 Ore gives. It comes 224 days after the Dutch approval and nine days after Finland.

Italy is a different size of win from the countries before it. All 11 earlier countries together have about 70.2 million residents. Italy alone has 58.9 million.

Populations are Eurostat's 1 Jan 2026 figures (EU total 451,990,314). The qualified-majority rule is from the EU Council. Sums and shares are my math.

The gap can't be closed with small countries. Germany is 18.5% of the EU, France 15.3% and Spain 11.0%. On my numbers, the 12 plus Germany and France reach 62.3%, still short, and that's only 14 countries. Adding Spain as well gets to 73.3%. So an EU-wide approval needs at least two of the three remaining big countries plus more of the smaller ones. Not everyone is leaning yes: Sweden's transport agency recommended in June that the EU reject the system while it could exceed speed limits (tracker record).

A national approval doesn't commit a country's vote, either. Finland's Traficom said its recognition "does not bind" its vote in the EU's Technical Committee on Motor Vehicles. No vote date has been published; our FSD Europe tracker lists December 2026 as a reported, unconfirmed opportunity.

What's announced and what's done

  • Done: Italy's ministry granted a provisional national approval (Salvini, 20 Nov).
  • Announced: Tesla says the rollout "begins soon." No date.
  • Reported: €99 a month (Italian press, citing Tesla). Not on Tesla's site at the time of writing.
  • Not decided: the EU-wide recognition, which Salvini's own post says is still being defined.

On the same day, South Korea's transport ministry said it couldn't even begin reviewing Tesla's request to unlock the system on Shanghai-built cars, because Tesla sent five pages, three of them cover sheets from the Dutch approval. I wrote that up separately.

My read

Italy is the first approval that moves the EU vote math in a real way, from about a sixth of the bloc to more than a quarter. Tesla's video was smart about it too: a car squeezing down Roman streets with people walking right in front of it is a harder test than a Dutch highway. For Italian owners the next date is when "soon" turns into an update on their cars. For everyone else it's Germany and France. Does Italy make you think Berlin and Paris follow before the EU votes, or do the big two hold out? Tell me below.

— Alex R
