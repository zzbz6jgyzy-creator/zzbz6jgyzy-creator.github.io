FLIGHT 13 / Block 3

First real Starlink V3s, ship lived through tip-over

Twenty Starlink V3 satellites flew the ship’s trajectory and burned up as planned. The ship splashed down and tipped over — and survived, so SpaceX got heat-shield and engine-bay photos. Booster 20 lost engines on the landing burn and hit the Gulf hard. Ship 40 was towed to Christmas Island, shipped home on Forte and is now in a Megabay at Starbase, the first Starship returned to its launch site.

SHIP

The spacecraft is also the upper stage.

After separation, the ship continues the mission: ascent, payload work, coast and return. It has to survive entry heating, control its descent and execute a terminal manoeuvre. A successful booster catch does not establish ship recovery.

STAGING

Stage separation, with the engines already burning.

Hot-staging starts the ship’s engines while the stages are still separating. The vented interstage provides a path for exhaust. On a test flight, the staging sequence and the later return of each vehicle are separate outcomes.

BOOSTER

The first stage has a return journey of its own.

Super Heavy provides the initial ascent, separates, turns back and manages its landing burn. Catch, controlled splashdown and vehicle loss are distinct recovery outcomes. The engine cluster below is an illustrative 33-engine arrangement.

Thirty-three engines. One coordinated ascent.
