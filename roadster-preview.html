Tesla ROADSTER

It flew.
Here is what is real.

Tesla's next-generation Roadster drove upside down, lifted over a line of Teslas on cold-gas thrusters and claimed 0 to 60 mph in 0.81 seconds. Production is targeted for 2028. Every number below says who said it.

The reveal, 15 October 2026

The night
it flew.

Tesla's event ran under half an hour and felt, as The Verge put it, more like a stunt show than a product reveal. These are the moments, and who said what about each one.

01

01 / The ramp

Upside down, on purpose.

The Roadster drove up a tall, C-shaped ramp and stopped on the inverted section, hanging upside down. Tesla says a fan and ground-sealing system pulls air out from under the car, giving enough suction to hold it there even when stationary.

Shown at the revealEngadget ↗The Verge ↗

02

02 / The flip

Off the top. A flip. A landing.

On a second run it left the top of the ramp and flipped, and its cold-gas thrusters eased it down to the ground. When the door opened, an Optimus robot was in the driver's seat. Engadget notes the stunts were programmed into the car, so nobody was driving.

Shown at the revealEngadget ↗TechCrunch ↗

03

03 / The hop

Over a line of Teslas.

TechCrunch reports the car also used its thrusters to lift itself over a Cybercab, a Cybertruck, a Semi, a Model Y and a Model 3 while moving. The Verge says Tesla's engineering VP Lars Moravy called one of the manoeuvres "a kick flip".

Shown at the revealTechCrunch ↗The Verge ↗

04

04 / The run

0.81 seconds.

During the event Tesla claimed a 0 to 60 mph run of 0.81 seconds, which TechCrunch reports as one second. Musk said the production car should improve on it. We have found no independent timing (as of 28 Oct 2026), and the start rules, rollout and surface all change what a time means.

Tesla's claimThe Verge ↗TechCrunch ↗

05

05 / The gap

And then no price.

There was no mention of price or release date. Musk said production begins in 2028 and, as The Verge quotes him, "This will be an actual car that's on the roads", though The Verge adds that the company later admitted it would not be road legal. Tesla says the Rocket trim is "not approved for public road use".

TargetThe Verge ↗TechCrunch ↗

Chapter 2 of 7

Beneath the surface

Extraordinary looks.
Serious engineering.

Six systems and the principle behind each. The diagram explains mechanisms; it is not Tesla CAD, a measured simulation or a prediction of performance.

Mechanism studySuction & ground sealingIllustrated principle, not a measured force model

System 01

Grip before speed.

A fan car can create downforce without waiting for high road speed.

How it works

Tesla describes powered fans that remove air from beneath the car, together with ground sealing. The resulting pressure difference can press the vehicle toward the surface. Unlike a wing, the basic mechanism does not need forward airflow.

Why it matters

More normal load can increase the tyre forces available for acceleration, braking and cornering. Surface sealing, fan power, debris, ride height and control behaviour all affect what a real system can deliver.

What this does, and does not, establish

Tesla says the system can hold the vehicle upside down at rest. The reveal is a controlled demonstration, not a published downforce map or an independent road-car test. Fan power, suction levels and the full operating envelope are not published here.

Tesla Roadster product page ↗

System 02

One shape. Different priorities.

Road, low-drag and high-downforce settings address different jobs.

How it works

Tesla describes deployable aero and a Track Mode that lowers the car and changes the suspension. Wing position and airflow management can trade drag against downforce as conditions change.

Why it matters

Reducing drag supports efficiency and high-speed running. Adding downforce can help tyres generate larger cornering and braking forces, with an energy and drag trade-off.

What this does, and does not, establish

These diagrams explain the principles. They are not a measured aerodynamic model, a wind-tunnel result or a guaranteed mode availability list for every configuration.

Tesla Roadster product page ↗

System 03

Force beyond tyre grip.

Stored gas expelled through nozzles produces a reaction force.

How it works

A cold-gas thruster uses stored pressure rather than combustion to expel gas. Rear-facing outlets can add forward force; other orientations can support vertical and rotational control. Tesla describes electrically actuated control and frequent adjustments.

Why it matters

This introduces forces that are not transmitted through the tyre contact patches. It also introduces a finite propellant supply, pressure-vessel mass, refill demands and a new control problem.

What this does, and does not, establish

Thrust magnitude, usable duration, pressure limits and refill time are not established by these images. Rocket is described as invite only and not approved for public road use; availability is regional.

Tesla Roadster product page ↗

System 04

Stored energy has a cost.

A composite pressure vessel is a structural system, not a battery specification.

How it works

Composite-overwrapped pressure vessels use an overwrap around a liner to contain high-pressure gas. The relevant design questions include usable gas mass, pressure, temperature, fatigue and how the vessels are supported and protected.

Why it matters

More stored gas can extend a thruster system’s operating time, but packaging, vehicle mass and repeated-cycle performance matter. A published tank volume alone cannot determine flight time.

What this does, and does not, establish

No tank pressure, certified life, refill time or system energy budget is verified here. A pressure-vessel layout is not a substitute for production safety documentation.

Tesla Roadster product page ↗

System 05

The driver stays central.

The physical controls and information layout are designed as one system.

How it works

Tesla describes a yoke designed for steer-by-wire, a central touchscreen and two smaller forward displays. Its current page also describes manual controls and a fixed seat position as part of its mass-reduction approach.

Why it matters

Steer-by-wire separates steering input sensing from a conventional mechanical linkage. Feedback, redundancy, calibration and the placement of information become central engineering questions.

What this does, and does not, establish

A cockpit image cannot verify steering feel, redundancy architecture, adjustable control ranges or the final production equipment list.

Tesla Roadster product page ↗

System 06

Every kilogram matters.

Materials, packaging and control choices affect the entire vehicle.

How it works

Tesla lists magnesium, titanium and carbon fibre among the materials used throughout the vehicle. Its published imagery shows the mechanical package; the exact contribution of each material is not quantified.

Why it matters

Lower mass can reduce the force and energy needed for a given manoeuvre. Stiffness, crash performance, durability, manufacturing and cost still need to be balanced.

What this does, and does not, establish

Production curb weight, battery capacity, charging performance and a validated range test are not established by this material list. The old 2017 battery and range promises are not treated as current specifications.

Tesla Roadster product page ↗

Straight from the engineer

What Lars Moravy
told Hagerty.

Tesla's VP of Vehicle Engineering walked Hagerty's Henry Catchpole round the car before the reveal. These are his claims, each with its source. We have found no independent test of any of them (as of 28 Oct 2026).

Rear-wheel drive, and more than a Plaid

Two rear motors, each with its own inverter, make over 1 MW and 1,400 Nm, which Moravy said is more power and torque than the three motors in a Model S Plaid. Hagerty converts that to 1,341 hp.

Tesla's claimSupercharged Daily ↗Hagerty ↗

Four fans seal the car to the road

Four fans at the rear, based on the heat-pump compressor motor at maybe 20 kW each, and four skirts that drop to seal the car. Moravy said the car can hold over two g in a corner at low speed.

Tesla's claimSupercharged Daily ↗

A wing moved by Optimus legs

The rear wing takes thousands of newtons of force, so Tesla used the leg actuators from the Optimus robot, with an added brake so they hold position. It switches between high-downforce and low-drag modes.

Tesla's claimSupercharged Daily ↗

Nitrogen, not fuel

On Thrust, a 200-litre carbon-wrapped pressure vessel holds nitrogen at up to 10,000 psi and feeds the rear thrusters, which Moravy said produce over 80 kilonewtons. Rocket adds a 400-litre tank and six more thrusters. (TechCrunch and The Verge describe the stored gas as compressed air.)

Tesla's claimSupercharged Daily ↗Hagerty ↗TechCrunch ↗

Thrust that fades, and slow refills

The thrust comes from pressure alone, so it fades as the tank empties. Hagerty says a full 600-litre refill takes six to seven hours today; Tesla's aim of about half an hour is a goal, not a result.

ReportedSupercharged Daily ↗

Chapter 5 of 7

The wait

Nine years,
one reveal.

The second-generation Roadster has been promised since 2017. This is what was said, when, and what happened to each date.

  1. Nov 2017

    The surprise

    Tesla showed the second-generation Roadster at the end of its Semi event, promising 0 to 60 mph in 1.9 seconds, over 250 mph, up to 620 miles from a 200 kWh battery, a $200,000 starting price and deliveries in 2020. Buyers put down $50,000, or $250,000 for one of 1,000 Founders Series cars.

    Tesla's claimThe Verge ↗TechCrunch ↗

  2. 2018

    Rocket thrusters, on Twitter

Chapter 6 of 7

Questions people ask

Straight
answers.

Short answers, each with the source behind it. Where a figure is a claim or a target, the answer says so.

How much does the Roadster cost?

Tesla has not announced a price. The $50,000 (Performance) and $250,000 (Thrust) figures are reservation deposits, which count toward the final price but are not it. Rocket is invite only.

Engadget ↗Autoblog ↗TechCrunch ↗The Verge ↗

When can I get one?

Musk said production starts in 2028. Tesla has given no delivery date, and its reservation agreement says it cannot guarantee when, or whether, a car will be delivered.

The Verge ↗TechCrunch ↗HotCars ↗

Can I get my deposit back?

Autoblog reports the deposit stays refundable until you sign the final purchase agreement, and that it cannot be transferred to someone else.

Autoblog ↗

Does it really fly?

In a controlled, programmed stunt it lifted over a line of Teslas and landed from a flip on cold-gas thrusters. Only the invite-only Rocket trim has the full thruster set, and Tesla's site says it is not approved for public road use.

Engadget ↗TechCrunch ↗The Verge ↗

How fast is it?

Tesla claims 0 to 60 mph in under 1.6 seconds for Performance, under 0.9 for Thrust and under 0.59 for the Rocket, and claimed 0.81 seconds on stage. Hagerty quotes 0.85 seconds for Thrust and a 0.55-second rollout figure for the Rocket. We have found no independent test of any of these (as of 28 Oct 2026).

The Verge ↗Hagerty ↗Supercharged Daily ↗

How much power does it make?

Hagerty reports over a megawatt (1,341 hp) and 1,400 Nm from two motors driving the rear wheels only, which Lars Moravy described as more than the three motors in a Model S Plaid.

Hagerty ↗Supercharged Daily ↗

What about range and charging?

Tesla's published target is more than 300 miles. Moravy told Hagerty the charging target is about 10 minutes from 15 to 80 percent. The battery size and the final range are not published, and we know of no certified range (as of 28 Oct 2026).

TechCrunch ↗The Verge ↗Supercharged Daily ↗Hagerty ↗

Is it road legal?

Tesla calls Performance "road ready", which is its positioning, not a certification. The Rocket carries the line "Not approved for public road use" on Tesla's site. Approvals depend on the market and the final configuration.

TechCrunch ↗The Verge ↗

How is it different from the 2017 Roadster?

In 2017 Tesla promised 0 to 60 mph in 1.9 seconds, over 250 mph, up to 620 miles from a 200 kWh battery, a $200,000 starting price and 2020 deliveries. It is now a two-seater (the Rocket seats one), quoted at more than 300 miles, with production targeted for 2028.

The Verge ↗TechCrunch ↗Hagerty ↗

What did SpaceX build and what did Tesla build?

Moravy told Hagerty the battery, powertrain, chassis, body, doors, seats and suction system are Tesla's, and that the thrust and flight packages were joint development with SpaceX. The Verge reported the suction system as supplied by SpaceX, which does not match his account.

Supercharged Daily ↗Hagerty ↗The Verge ↗
