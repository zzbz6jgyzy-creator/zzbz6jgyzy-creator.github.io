Tesla ROADSTER

It flew.
Here is what is real.

Tesla's next-generation Roadster drove upside down, lifted over a line of Teslas on cold-gas thrusters and claimed 0 to 60 mph in 0.81 seconds. Production is targeted for 2028. Every number below says who said it.

The reveal, 15 October 2026

The night
it flew.

Tesla's event ran under half an hour and felt, as The Verge put it, more like a stunt show than a product reveal. These are the moments, and who said what about each one.

01 / The ramp

Upside down, on purpose.

The Roadster drove up a tall, C-shaped ramp and stopped on the inverted section, hanging upside down. Tesla says a fan and ground-sealing system pulls air out from under the car, giving enough suction to hold it there even when stationary.

Shown at the revealEngadget ↗The Verge ↗

02 / The flip

Off the top. A flip. A landing.

On a second run it left the top of the ramp and flipped, and its cold-gas thrusters eased it down to the ground. When the door opened, an Optimus robot was in the driver's seat. Engadget notes the stunts were programmed into the car, so nobody was driving.

Shown at the revealEngadget ↗TechCrunch ↗

03 / The hop

Over a line of Teslas.

TechCrunch reports the car also used its thrusters to lift itself over a Cybercab, a Cybertruck, a Semi, a Model Y and a Model 3 while moving. Tesla's engineering VP Lars Moravy said the car carries 10 thrusters fed by 600 litres of compressed air, and that the tanks can be recharged.

Shown at the revealTechCrunch ↗The Verge ↗

04 / The run

0.81 seconds.

During the event Tesla claimed a 0 to 60 mph run of 0.81 seconds; TechCrunch rounds it to one second. Musk said the production car should improve on it. We have found no independent timing, and the start rules, rollout and surface all change what a time means.

Tesla's claimThe Verge ↗TechCrunch ↗Tesla’s timed reveal run ↗

05 / The gap

And then no price.

There was no mention of price or release date. Musk said production begins in 2028 and described the demonstrated car as one that will be on the roads. The Verge adds that the company later admitted the flying version would not be road legal.

TargetThe Verge ↗TechCrunch ↗

The money and the wait

A reservation
is not a price.

Tesla has not announced what the Roadster will cost. What it has published is what it costs to join the queue, and the terms of that queue.

$50,000

$5,000 at reservation, then $45,000 within 10 days.

Questions people ask

Straight
answers.

Short answers, each with the source behind it. Where a figure is a claim or a target, the answer says so.

How much does the Roadster cost?

Tesla has not announced a price. The $50,000 (Performance) and $250,000 (Thrust) figures are reservation deposits, which count toward the final price but are not it. Rocket is invite only.

Engadget ↗Autoblog ↗The Verge ↗

When can I get one?

Musk said production starts in 2028. Tesla has given no delivery date, and its reservation agreement says it cannot guarantee when, or whether, a car will be delivered.

The Verge ↗TechCrunch ↗HotCars ↗

Can I get my deposit back?

Autoblog reports the deposit stays refundable until you sign the final purchase agreement, and that it cannot be transferred to someone else.

Autoblog ↗

Does it really fly?

In a controlled, programmed stunt it lifted over a line of Teslas and landed from a flip on cold-gas thrusters. Only the invite-only Rocket trim has the full thruster set, and Tesla's site says it is not approved for public road use.

Engadget ↗TechCrunch ↗Supercharged Daily ↗

How fast is it?

Tesla claims 0 to 60 mph in under 1.6 seconds for Performance, under 0.9 for Thrust and under 0.59 for the Rocket, and claimed 0.81 seconds on stage. Hagerty quotes 0.85 seconds for Thrust and a 0.55-second rollout figure for the Rocket. None of these has been independently tested.

The Verge ↗Hagerty ↗Supercharged Daily ↗

How much power does it make?

Hagerty reports over a megawatt (1,341 hp) and 1,400 Nm from two motors driving the rear wheels only, which Lars Moravy described as more than the three motors in a Model S Plaid.

Hagerty ↗Supercharged Daily ↗

What about range and charging?

Tesla's published target is more than 300 miles. Hagerty reports a charging target of about 10 minutes from 15 to 80 percent. The battery size and the final range are not published, and no range has been certified.

TechCrunch ↗The Verge ↗Supercharged Daily ↗

Is it road legal?

Tesla calls Performance "road ready", which is its positioning, not a certification. The Rocket carries the line "Not approved for public road use" on Tesla's site. Approvals depend on the market and the final configuration.

TechCrunch ↗The Verge ↗Supercharged Daily ↗

How is it different from the 2017 Roadster?

In 2017 Tesla promised 0 to 60 mph in 1.9 seconds, over 250 mph, up to 620 miles from a 200 kWh battery, a $200,000 starting price and 2020 deliveries. It is now a two-seater, quoted at more than 300 miles, with production targeted for 2028.

The Verge ↗TechCrunch ↗Hagerty ↗

What did SpaceX build and what did Tesla build?

Moravy told Hagerty the battery, powertrain, chassis, body, doors, seats and suction system are Tesla's. The thrust and flight packages were joint development with SpaceX.

Supercharged Daily ↗Hagerty ↗
