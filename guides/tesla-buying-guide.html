A better way to begin

Start with your life.
Then choose the car.

A useful shortlist begins with real journeys, passengers, parking and charging. A headline range or monthly payment is only one part of the decision.

01 / Your routine

What must it do?

Think about passengers, bulky loads, your usual parking spot and the difficult trip you make a few times a year.

02 / Your charging

Where does it refill?

Price the charging routine you can actually use: home, work, public AC or rapid charging. Convenience is part of the cost.

03 / Your budget

What does ownership cost?

Include insurance, tyres, electricity, finance and likely resale. A low payment can hide a long term or a large final bill.

Choose a trim deliberately.

Start with the features and journey margin you need. Check the actual drive layout, wheels, seating, suspension, cabin equipment, towing approval and battery warranty—not just the badge.

Performance wheels and tyres can change ride, running costs and range. Buy the equipment you will use, then check its replacement cost.

Use the right range number.

EPA and WLTP use different test procedures. A converted mile figure is still the same rating standard; it does not become another test result.

For a difficult trip, think about speed, temperature, elevation, loaded weight, charger access and reserve. Rated range is not a door-to-door winter itinerary.

New, inventory or used?

A factory order, an inventory car and a used vehicle can have different equipment, timing, history and cover. Compare actual itemised offers rather than a generic starting price.

A used Model S or X is not simply a larger Model 3/Y. Research the generation, hardware, fitted options and likely repair exposure. A good independent inspection can be more useful than a seller’s confident description.

Check the actual warranty.

US reference: basic coverage is four years/50,000 miles. Battery and drive-unit coverage is eight years with mileage limits of 100,000, 120,000 or 150,000 miles depending on the named model/trim, with a stated 70% retention threshold.

Tesla’s Certified Pre-Owned cover generally adds one year/10,000 miles after basic cover; lease terms can differ. Remaining battery cover is separate. The vehicle’s supplied warranty and local terms control.

Read Tesla’s US warranty

Verify software entitlements.

Check the installed software and hardware on the exact vehicle. A badge, a seller’s old screenshot or “capable” in a listing is not a promise that a feature is available in your country.

Active driver supervision remains required for FSD (Supervised). Subscriptions, promotions and some benefits may not transfer. Confirm the purchase or subscription status with the seller and Tesla.

Check regional evidence

Treat incentives as a separate check.

US federal new, used and qualified-commercial clean-vehicle credits are unavailable for vehicles acquired after 30 September 2025. Earlier qualifying acquisitions have their own conditions.

Do not extrapolate this to another country or assume a local, state or utility incentive applies to your situation. Verify the current scheme and your eligibility before putting a saving into the budget.

Read the IRS guidance

Make the account handover part of the deal.

For a third-party purchase, claim ownership through the Tesla app and follow Tesla’s document process. Confirm the previous owner’s access has been removed and your account has the right permissions.

Do not assume every charging credit, subscription or paid upgrade stays with a car. Check that separately before purchase.
