Which Tesla should you buy in 2026?

From @ALJR86Ireland · UK · US

Disclosure: this guide contains my Tesla referral link and affiliate links to accessory shops. If you use them I may earn a credit or commission at no extra cost to you. Prices below were checked on Tesla’s own regional pages on 14 Oct 2026 and change often, so always confirm the final price in Tesla’s checkout.

The short answer

For most people it is a choice between the two cheapest cars Tesla sells: the Model 3 Rear-Wheel Drive saloon and the Model Y Rear-Wheel Drive SUV, which Tesla now sells as the entry “Standard” trims. Buy the Model 3 if you want the lowest price, the most range per euro and a sportier car. Buy the Model Y if you need the bigger boot, a higher seat or more towing.

Grants and incentives

  • Ireland: the SEAI grant is still open. Private buyers get up to €3,500 off a new electric car, applied by the dealer. Since 1 August 2026 the car’s full price, including options, must be €50,000 or less (it was €60,000). It does not apply to used cars. The separate ICE2EV scrappage top-up is closed because its €10 million budget ran out. Electric cars pay the lowest motor tax, €120 a year. Sources: SEAI grant amounts, SEAI EV grants, Dáil answer, 11 Jun 2026.
  • UK: the Electric Car Grant gives £1,500 or £3,750 off approved new cars priced at £37,000 or less (up to £42,000 for some variants). No Tesla appears on the eligible lists published by Which? and RAC. Rules: GOV.UK.
  • US: the federal new and used clean-vehicle tax credits ended for cars acquired after 30 Sept 2025, and the home-charger credit only covers equipment placed in service before 1 July 2026. Check your state and utility for local rebates. Source: IRS.

New or used?

A new car gets you the current “Highland” Model 3 or new-shape Model Y, the full warranty and, in Ireland, the SEAI grant, which used cars cannot get. A used Tesla avoids the wait (a new Irish Model 3 ordered today shows Feb–Mar 2027 delivery) and the early depreciation. If you buy used, check which version it is, how much battery and drive-unit warranty is left on the Tesla warranty page, and whether any paid driver-assistance package is tied to the car. Tesla also sells used and demo cars directly through its own inventory pages.

Home charging

Charging at home overnight is the cheapest and easiest way to own an EV. Tesla Ireland lists its Wall Connector at €535 and the portable Mobile Connector at €205 in the configurator. In Ireland, SEAI’s home charger grant pays up to €300 towards a smart charger on its register (the Tesla Wall Connector is listed). You need off-street parking at your home, a Safe Electric registered electrician, and you must not start work before your letter of offer arrives. SEAI recommends an electrical survey first, because older homes may need upgraded wiring.

FSD and driver assistance in Europe

In the US, Tesla sells Full Self-Driving (Supervised) as a $99-a-month subscription. In Ireland and the UK, Tesla’s pages say its European system, Tesla Assisted Driving, “is not yet available”, and offer a €99/£99-a-month Basic package of Autosteer, Autopark, Summon and similar features that still need you to supervise. Eight EU countries, starting with the Netherlands, had approved it as of 12 Oct, and Ireland is still under review. Follow it country by country on the FSD Europe tracker.
