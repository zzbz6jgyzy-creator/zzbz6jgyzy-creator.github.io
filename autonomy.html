Evidence first.

FSD, Robotaxi and the space between a promise and proof. Understand who is driving, what is permitted and what the numbers actually establish.

Start with the distinction

One brand.
Different responsibilities.

The same vocabulary can describe a customer feature, a legal permission or a commercial service. Ask three questions before drawing a conclusion.

01

Who drives?

Is a human supervising the road, or is the system performing the driving task? What human assistance remains?

02

Where and when?

Which roads, operating conditions, jurisdiction and service boundary does the evidence cover?

03

What establishes it?

A manufacturer statement, regulator record, observed trip and statistical result answer different questions.

Customer feature / supervised

The car assists.
The driver supervises.

Tesla’s FSD (Supervised) guidance keeps an attentive human responsible for driving. Product naming, steering capability and a smooth demo do not change that responsibility.

  • Driver continuously supervises
  • Driver must intervene when needed
  • Feature eligibility depends on the actual car

Read your owner’s manual and local Tesla guidance for the vehicle’s actual features.

Separate records / not automatic steps

Permission. Listing.
Delivery. Service.

A record in one column is not evidence that all the other columns are satisfied. Rules also differ by jurisdiction and operating model.

Legal permission

What is authorised?

Read the operator, category, conditions and authority.

Market listing

What is offered?

A manufacturer describes availability in a market.

Vehicle delivery

What reached a car?

Check the actual hardware, software and installation.

Commercial service

What can a rider book?

Check the vehicle, trip boundary and service conditions.

Assumptions & derivation

Before you repeat “safer than”

Match the comparison.

01

Same event? Read the outcome and severity threshold.

02

Same exposure? Match miles, dates and supervision mode.

03

Same conditions? Compare roads, locations and time patterns.

04

Same capture? Check reporting rules, missing events and revisions.

NHTSA cautions that reporting requirements, telemetry access and exposure vary. Its SGO counts are not an adjusted safety league table.

Open the regulator’s limitations
Statistical model: what the zero-event result means

For an illustrative Poisson event count with constant rate λ and exposure E, the chance of zero events is exp(−λE). Setting that probability to 0.05 gives a one-sided 95% upper rate bound of −ln(0.05)/E, approximately 2.996/E. Multiply by 1,000,000 for events per million miles.

This model assumes independent events, a constant rate over the stated exposure, a fixed event definition, correct mileage and complete ascertainment. Real driving can violate these assumptions. The output is not a posterior probability that the rate lies below the bound and does not establish equivalence to another population.

The lab only computes this bound for a zero-event sample. It does not calculate general confidence intervals, causal effects or an individual trip’s risk.

Our editorial method

Traceable, bounded and revisable.

  1. Identify the precise claim, vehicle, place and operating mode.
  2. Read the primary record and retain the distinction between statement, permission and observation.
  3. Describe the result within that scope. Keep unresolved questions visible.
  4. Record the review date; update the desk when the underlying evidence changes.

This desk is curated editorially. It does not poll regulators or vehicles in real time. The specialist trackers contain the changing lists and records. Manufacturer statements remain attributed; our interpretation is not official approval.
