How to switch

How to switch electricity supplier in Ireland

It's free, it's done online in a few minutes, and your power isn't cut off. Here's what to do, with the official sources.

  1. Check your current contract and exit fees

    Find out when your contract ends. There's no charge for switching at the end of a contract, but there may be an early exit fee if you leave a fixed-term contract midway through. bonkers.ie says this is usually about €50 per fuel on a standard 12-month plan, though some plans charge more.

    Ask your current supplier if they'll offer you a better deal first. They'll often try to keep you.

    Sources: CRU: Switch energy supplier · bonkers.ie: energy cancellation fees

  2. Have your details ready

    • Your MPRN (Meter Point Reference Number): the unique 11-digit number for your electricity connection. It always starts with 10 and is printed at the top of your electricity bill (top right on most bills). It isn't shown on the meter.
    • An up-to-date meter reading, so your final and first bills are accurate.
    • Bank details (IBAN) for direct debit, and your Eircode. Many discounts need it, for example SSE's 15% (direct debit + eBill).
    • A smart meter. EV and dynamic plans are time-of-use tariffs that run on a smart meter.

    Sources: ESB Networks: how to find my MPRN · CRU · Energia switching guide

  3. Compare and choose a plan

    Look at the EV rate and the cheap window length, day rate and standing charge. A cheap EV rate can be cancelled out by a high standing charge if you don't drive much. Use our plan comparison and calculator, then cross-check on bonkers.ie (CRU-accredited) or switcher.ie.

  4. Sign up with the new supplier. They handle the rest

    Sign up on the new supplier's website (links below). You don't need to contact your old supplier: the new supplier tells them you're leaving, and the transfer goes through ESB Networks' Meter Registration System Operator. Nobody needs to visit and your supply isn't interrupted.

    Sources: Citizens Information · CRU · bonkers.ie

  5. 14-day cooling-off period

    You have 14 days to change your mind and cancel the switch with no penalty. For contracts signed at your door or during an unsolicited visit, it's 30 days.

    Sources: CRU · Citizens Information

  6. Wait for the switch to complete: allow up to about 3 weeks

    bonkers.ie says most switches complete within 5–10 working days, but it can take up to four weeks, especially if information like a meter reading is missing. The CRU says the transfer is carried out once the cooling-off period has ended, so plan for a couple of weeks.

    You'll get a welcome letter from your new supplier and a final bill from your old one. Then set your car or charger to the new plan's cheap window.

    Sources: bonkers.ie: how to switch · CRU
