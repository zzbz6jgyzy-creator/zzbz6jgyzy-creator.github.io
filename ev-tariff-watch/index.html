      Yearly charging cost by plan

      How we calculate this (assumptions)
      • Energy: kWh per year = km × efficiency ÷ 100 × (1 + charging losses). Home kWh = that × share charged at home. Public charging isn't costed.
      • EV plans: all home charging is assumed to fit in the plan's cheap window at its headline rate (incl. VAT and the advertised new-customer discount). If your nightly need is more than the window can deliver at your charger power, we flag it. The extra kWh would cost the plan's night/day rate, which we don't add.
      • Not included: standing charges and the rest of your household's electricity. EV plans often have higher standing charges and day rates, so a low EV rate can be cancelled out for low-mileage drivers. Check the full plan.
      • Dynamic estimate: base night rate (incl VAT) + wholesale price × 1.09 VAT. Bord Gáis's Smart Dynamic T&Cs confirm the dynamic part is the Day-Ahead Market price for each half hour (capped at 50c/kWh). We use the cheapest night base we found (Electric Ireland, 8.52c). Real costs vary night to night.
      • 24-hour rate: Electric Ireland Home Electric+ Saver 16% at 31.30c, an example of a normal flat rate.
      • "After 12 Oct" uses all announced rises (SSE 5 Oct, Bord Gáis 9 Oct, Energia 12 Oct), including figures we derived (marked).

      Dynamic vs night rate: which wins for EV charging?

      Since 1 June 2026 the five biggest suppliers must offer a dynamic tariff, where the price changes every half hour with the wholesale market. Here's how that stacks up against a fixed EV night rate.

      At 2–5am, a typical EV charging time

      Estimate = supplier's night base rate + 16.38c average wholesale (4 Sep – 3 Oct 2026) + 9% VAT. On a windy night wholesale can drop to near zero (avg 2–5am on 19 Sep 2026: 0.79c). On a calm night it can top 20c.

      In plain English

      • Night rate (EV plan): one known low price in a fixed window, e.g. 2–5am. Simple. Set your car or charger and forget it.
      • Dynamic: a fixed base rate plus the wholesale price for each half hour, published the day before. Cheaper on windy nights, dearer when gas sets the price.
      • Right now Irish wholesale prices are high (Sept 2026 average about 18c/kWh before VAT), so a good EV night rate beats our dynamic estimate on most nights.
      • Dynamic needs a smart meter with a strong signal (CTF 4) and suits people who'll watch prices or use a charger that follows them.

      The trend: over the past 12 months, perfectly timed charging in the cheapest 3 hours each night averaged about 20c/kWh on a dynamic tariff (our backtest). But overnight wholesale prices have roughly doubled since then, with the 2–5am average rising from about 7c last October to about 16c in September 2026 before VAT. That puts today's figure at 26c or more. Either way, it's more than double a good EV night rate.
