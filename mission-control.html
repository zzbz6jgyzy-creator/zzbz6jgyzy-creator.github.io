BEHIND THE VIEW

A world worth looking closer at.

Light that follows the present.

“Right now” calculates the Sun’s position from the current UTC time. Day and night follow geography as you move the camera. The orbiting camera is for exploration; it is not Earth’s real rotation speed. Day study and Night study are artistic lighting presets. Local civil time is shown for featured places; other coordinates show UTC. The sunlight calculation is approximate and is not for navigation.

Latest clouds, with a visible date.

Clouds come from Matt Eason’s Live Cloud Maps, derived from EUMETSAT satellite composites. The service updates about every three hours; it is not an instantaneous picture of the whole Earth. The displayed date is the image’s publication time, not a single global observation time. Contains modified EUMETSAT data.

Infrared and visible observations are processed into a visual cloud mask. Some regions may have gaps. The provider fills polar areas by mirroring other latitudes; this view hides that fill beyond 67.5° north and south, with a soft transition. Missing polar clouds do not mean clear skies. This is an exploration view, not a weather forecast. If the feed fails, the page identifies the previous image or illustrative clouds. Images older than 12 hours are marked as older; images older than three days are rejected.

From a globe to a detailed map.

The ground view uses MapLibre, OpenFreeMap and OpenStreetMap, with Mapzen open elevation. Roads, terrain and building footprints are mapped data; building heights depend on available records and may be approximated. It is a styled map, not photographic or real-time street imagery. Map lighting is illustrative. Terrain is shown at 1.15× height for clarity. Detail and coverage vary.

The Satellite imagery link opens the same location in Google Maps in a new tab or app. Its imagery is separate from this globe and may be historical. No Google API, paid tiles or billing account is needed for this page.

Detail that adapts.

Auto balances sharpness, data use and performance. High requests 4K surface detail; Ultra requests 8K surface and cloud imagery when the device supports it. GPU limits can reduce resolution. Maps load only when opened; pause the globe whenever you like. Reduced-motion preferences are respected.
