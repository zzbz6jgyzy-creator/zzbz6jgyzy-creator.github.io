06

What Tesla says about safety

Tesla's own published crash-rate comparisons, what each is measured against, and what we could not verify.

What counts as a collision

A major collision is one severe enough to deploy an airbag or seatbelt pretensioner, or to fire the high-voltage battery pyro fuse. Tesla counts a crash as an FSD crash if FSD was active at any point in the five seconds before impact.

A year earlier, as the same outlet gives it, Tesla reported one major collision every 5.1 million miles with FSD, 2.3 million for manual driving with Active Safety and 927,000 without.

Reported Not a Tesla App, 19 Feb 2026 →

By 19 February Tesla had reported one major collision every 5.30 million miles with FSD (Supervised) engaged, against 2.18 million for manually driven Teslas with Active Safety and 0.66 million for the U.S. average. Dividing, that is 2.4× the first and 8.0× the second Our estimate. These are Tesla's rates for Tesla's groups, not a regulator's finding.

Europe: the dashboard Tesla gave regulators

As EVwire reports Tesla's EU dashboard: 12 collisions with FSD in 102.1 million km of customer driving in five countries (10 April to 24 August 2026), against 627 in 1,295.7 million km for manually driven Teslas with Active Safety on. That is 4.1 times fewer, a comparison with Tesla's own cars, not average drivers. Tesla's July update said 5.2 times after 65 million km.

Reported EVwire, 2 Sep 2026 → · Drive Tesla Canada, 1 Sep 2026 → · Automotive World, 2 Sep 2026 →

Read it with these caveats

  • Every figure here is Tesla's own, as an outlet describes it. We have not compared it with Tesla's original report or dashboard (the only thing we recorded from Tesla's safety page is its market list, 26 Oct), so Tesla may have published newer figures since. Reported Not a Tesla App, 19 Feb 2026 → EVwire, 2 Sep 2026 →
  • Our reading: the groups differ and the figures are not adjusted for driver or vehicle age. In the U.S. table the average is every vehicle on the road and the no-active-safety group is pre-2014 Teslas. The EU comparison is against Teslas with Active Safety on, not average drivers, and it mixes road types: EVwire reports 5.1× on highways (3 collisions in 65.5 million km against 137 in 578.1) and 2.7× off them (9 in 36.6 against 490 in 717.6). EVwire, 2 Sep 2026 →
  • The reports disagree about dates. EVwire says the 4.1× covers customer driving from 10 April to 24 August 2026. Drive Tesla Canada says every dated window in the dashboard shared with EU states in April closes in 2025, and Automotive World's headline calls the newly published data nine months out of date. We have not opened the dashboard to settle it. Reported EVwire, 2 Sep 2026 → Drive Tesla Canada, 1 Sep 2026 → Automotive World, 2 Sep 2026 →
  • These are Tesla's rates, as reported. They are not a regulator's finding and we draw no safety conclusion from them. FSD (Supervised) always needs an attentive driver.

How to read the numbers

Every figure on this page carries one of these labels, a date and a source.

  • Disclosed Tesla or a regulator said it in a document we opened.
  • Reported A journalist, analyst or tracker found it, or Tesla published it and an outlet describes it; we have not compared it with the original. We quote the outlet.
  • Our estimate Our own arithmetic on disclosed numbers. Shown dashed or marked.
  • Approx. from chart Read off a chart Tesla published without exact labels.
  • Target A stated aim or date. Not a result.
  • Our expectation Our guess at when a number will next change. Not a schedule Tesla has given.
