How a booster catch works

The returning booster slows and aligns with the tower so its catch fittings can meet the arms. Once supported, the booster’s weight is carried by the tower rather than landing legs.

By Alex RReference review 23 Oct 2026

Explore diagramKey ideasSources

A catch is a sequence of positioning, braking and alignment. The two views below focus on those relationships, with an enlarged view of the catch area.

Explore the sequence

From approach to support

Five stages
  1. 1Elevation: approach to the tower
  2. 2Plan: alignment from above
  3. 3Catch area: load transfers to the arms

  1. 01 · Return toward the launch site

    After separation, the booster’s return sequence brings it back toward the launch area. This diagram begins with the approach; it does not plot the full boostback trajectory.

  2. 02 · Control the approach

    The booster must reach the catch area with the required position and orientation. Compare the elevation with the overhead view: arriving at the right height alone is not enough.

  3. 03 · Slow for the catch

    The landing burn reduces the booster’s speed as it approaches the arms. The illustrated flame marks that phase, without assigning an engine count or a fixed burn duration.

  4. 04 · Meet the catch fittings

    The booster’s catch fittings and the arms must line up. The red markers identify the relationship being explained, not the tolerances of an actual flight.

  5. 05 · The tower carries the load

    Once supported by the arms, the booster hangs at the tower. The final view highlights the support path and turns off the landing-burn illustration.

Why put the landing hardware on the ground?

A tower catch moves some recovery infrastructure off the flying vehicle. It also makes the return depend on a particular facility and a tightly coordinated approach. That is the central engineering trade.

A demonstrated milestone

SpaceX’s fifth Starship flight demonstrated the first Super Heavy tower catch. The official film below is the reference for the broad sequence; the illustration is an explanation of that sequence, not a reconstruction of its telemetry. [source]

A clearer picture

Common misunderstandings

The arms are not a net

The mechanism depends on alignment with the booster’s catch area. A diagram of two arms around a cylinder is not a description of the complete guidance and control system.

A booster catch does not prove a ship catch

Starship and Super Heavy are different vehicles. A completed booster catch should not be presented as confirmation of a future ship catch or an unannounced flight plan.

Plain English

Key terms

Boostback
The burn that redirects the booster toward its return area.
Elevation
A side view that shows height and vertical relationships.
Plan view
An overhead view that shows lateral alignment.
