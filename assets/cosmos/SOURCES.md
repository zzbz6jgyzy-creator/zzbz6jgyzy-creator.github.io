# Earth and space imagery and data

- **16K Earth day**: NASA Earth Observatory / Reto Stöckli, Blue Marble Next Generation, July 2004. Original 21600×10800 JPEG; resized to 16384×8192 JPEG. Historical composite, not a live photograph. Manual Studio selection only, on capable large-screen devices.
- **8K Earth night**: NASA Earth Observatory / Joshua Stevens; Suomi NPP VIIRS, Miguel Román / NASA GSFC; Black Marble 2016. Historical composite, resized and converted to WebP.
- **Earth normal and ocean-specular masks**: Solar System Scope / INOVE, NASA-derived, [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Resized to 4K and converted to WebP. The normal strength is deliberately restrained for a space-view relief study.
- **Sky panorama**: **ESO/S. Brunier**, [The Milky Way panorama, eso0932a](https://www.eso.org/public/images/eso0932a/), 2009, [CC BY 4.0](https://www.eso.org/public/outreach/copyright/). Published 6000×3000 image resized to 4096×2048 WebP and projected in galactic coordinates; exposure changes in the shader. Not AI-generated. This is an Earth/Sun-view historical panorama, and fades out as the camera leaves the Solar System. It is not a photograph taken from outside our galaxy.
- **Moon surface**: NASA's Scientific Visualization Studio / LRO / LROC / ASU, [CGI Moon Kit](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4720/), 2025 colour map. 4K sRGB 16-bit TIFF converted to 8-bit WebP. Source documentation identifies polar albedo fill, small inpainted dropouts, exposure/white-balance adjustments and its visualisation purpose.
- **Other planet surfaces and Saturn rings**: [Solar System Scope / INOVE](https://www.solarsystemscope.com/textures/), NASA-derived, [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Published 2K images converted to WebP. These are visualisation maps: colours are enhanced and unmapped gaps may be filled. They are not current spacecraft camera views.
- **Stars**: HYG v4.1, David Nash / Astronomy Nexus, based on Hipparcos, Yale Bright Star and Gliese. [Source archive](https://github.com/astronexus/HYG-Database), [current project](https://codeberg.org/astronexus/hyg). The modified `stars.json` database is **CC BY-SA 4.0**. It selects apparent magnitude ≤6.5 or distance ≤40 pc, excludes absent/dubious distance sentinels ≥100000 pc, rounds values and retains selected fields. Epoch and equinox J2000. Velocities are applied linearly to the viewing year; measurement uncertainties and incomplete coverage remain. Marker brightness/size is illustrative.
- **Ephemerides**: [Astronomy Engine 2.1.19](https://github.com/cosinekitty/astronomy), Don Cross, **MIT** (licence is vendored). Calculated Sun, Moon and planet positions; no network ephemeris API or paid key. Physical lengths use the IAU astronomical unit, mean body radii, parsecs and light-years. Body orientation uses the library's IAU rotation-axis model.
- **Milky Way outer view**: original procedural **structural illustration**, not a catalogue of every star. Roughly 100,000-light-year stellar disk, a central bar/bulge, spiral structure and Sun at approximately 8.2 kpc (26,700 light-years) from the centre, with 20.8 pc height offset. IAU galactic orientation is transformed to the J2000 Earth scene. The outer population is synthetic and arm structure schematic. [NASA galaxy overview](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/galaxies/), [NASA Chandra Milky Way](https://chandra.si.edu/xray_sources/milky_way.html). Labels are enlarged for visibility; body radii and orbital distances are physical.

All raster resizing uses Lanczos. `manifest.json` contains exact source URLs, output dimensions, sizes and SHA-256 digests. No original texture was upscaled.

## Current infrared layer

**Contains modified EUMETSAT data.** Source: [EUMETSAT EUMETView](https://view.eumetsat.int/), public WMS layer `mumi:worldcloudmap_ir108`. The actual default frame time is read from GetCapabilities and pinned in GetMap. A three-hour composite is not instantaneous global weather. Infrared brightness is mapped to a cloud-opacity proxy; low cloud may be missed and cold surfaces can resemble cloud. Polar areas beyond ±67.5° are hidden rather than filled. This is not a forecast or a complete cloud classification.

The previous Matt Eason image endpoint loaded successfully, but successive October 2026 archives had identical SHA-256 digests and the endpoint supplied no image time. This version therefore stops using it as current weather. No HTTP retrieval time or archive download timestamp is presented as an observation time.

No NASA, ESO, EUMETSAT or catalogue-provider endorsement is implied.
