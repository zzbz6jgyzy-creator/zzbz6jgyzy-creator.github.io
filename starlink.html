The satellite lifecycle

Different numbers. Different questions.

  1. Launch & deployment

    13,096 carried on launch attempts; 20 failed to reach orbit.

  2. Climbing & positioning

    85 ascending and 202 drifting in the count source. These are not necessarily out of service.

  3. Operational shells

    10,022 in classified orbital shells. Working is a broader category: 11,193.

  4. Retirement & reentry

    110 in disposal; 1,869 have reentered. Disposal and reentry are different events.

      How often does this tracker update?

      Counts and orbital data refresh every eight hours. A source may publish less often or not respond, so the page keeps the last valid data and shows each source’s own date.

      Are these satellite positions live?

      They are calculated using SGP4 and published orbital elements. The initial view uses the saved source epoch; the explorer can propagate to the current time or a selected offset. These are not GPS measurements or live operator telemetry. Prediction uncertainty grows as the elements age.

      What does “working” mean?

      It is McDowell’s classification for in-orbit spacecraft excluding screened or dead/decaying objects. It does not confirm that every spacecraft is delivering user traffic. An operational shell describes orbital placement and is a different category.

      Can the sky finder identify a light with certainty?

      No. It offers geometric candidates near your phone’s pointing direction. Compass errors, stale orbital elements and objects outside the Starlink catalogue can cause a mismatch. An overhead candidate may be invisible to the naked eye.

      What is included in the count?

      McDowell’s published Starlink totals, including the classified generations shown here. The Tintin prototypes and V0.9 are retained in the source totals; both have zero remaining in orbit. Starshield and bracketed simulator payloads are excluded. Failed orbital attempts are included in launched totals but excluded from the reentered figure.

      Where is my location or saved list stored?

      The saved satellite list stays in this browser. The sky finder stores a selected location locally and needs no account. City search sends the query to Open-Meteo; device location and compass permissions are requested only when you use their controls. The optional 3D view loads external imagery.
