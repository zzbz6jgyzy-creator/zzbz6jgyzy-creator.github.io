Tesla / Cybercab guide

Cybercab, checked against the sources

Per NHTSA, Tesla’s purpose-built robotaxi lacks a permanently attached steering wheel and pedals. By Tesla’s own deck it started carrying Austin riders on 3 September 2026. This guide separates what Tesla has disclosed from what has only been reported and what is still a target.

Reviewed 27 Oct 2026 · Live fleet counters and cities →

01

State of play

Six numbers that matter, each with its label, date and source.

  • Disclosed Tesla or a regulator said it in a document we opened.
  • Target A stated aim or date. Not a result.
  • Reported A journalist’s or reviewer’s finding. Tesla has not confirmed it.

Cybercab rides in Austin

3 Sep 2026start date

Tesla's Q3 deck: "We started providing Robotaxi rides using Cybercabs on September 3 in Austin." Public booking opened the next day, per Not a Tesla App.

Disclosed As of 21 Oct 2026 Tesla, 21 Oct 2026 → · Not a Tesla App, 4 Sep 2026 →

>125,000vehicles a year

Tesla's own footnote: "Installed capacity ≠ current production rate." Tesla has not published how many Cybercabs it has built.

Disclosed As of 21 Oct 2026 Tesla, 21 Oct 2026 →

Cybercabs serving Austin

300+company claim

Tesla's Robotaxi account posted "300+ Cybercabs serving Austin – an 8x increase in 30 days". It is a count of vehicles in service, not of deliveries, and Tesla gave no breakdown.

Disclosed As of 10 Oct 2026 Tesla Robotaxi on X, 10 Oct 2026 →

NHTSA audit of the certification

AQ26002open since 3 Sep

NHTSA opened an audit query into the process Tesla relied on to certify a car that lacks permanently attached manual controls. Not a Tesla App reports 21 requests, due 30 Oct after a 30-day extension.

Disclosed As of 8 Oct 2026 NHTSA, 3 Sep 2026 → · Not a Tesla App, 8 Oct 2026 →

Cybercabs for customers

By end of 2026stated aim

On the Q3 call Lars Moravy said: "we're trying to get Cybercabs to customers by the end of this year, 2026". Today every Cybercab built goes to Tesla's own fleet, and Musk said most will stay Tesla-owned.

Target As of 21 Oct 2026 The Motley Fool, 22 Oct 2026 →

Cost target

<$30,000set in Oct 2024

At the 2024 reveal Musk said he "expects the cost to be below $30,000", per The Drive. Tesla has published no price since.

Target As of 10 Oct 2024 The Drive, 11 Oct 2024 →

04

What “more than 125,000 a year” means

Installed capacity is not output. This table is arithmetic you can move, not a forecast.

More than 125,000 a year is what Tesla says Giga Texas is set up to build Disclosed Tesla, 21 Oct 2026 →. Tesla says that is not the current production rate, and has published no Cybercab-specific output. The 300+ cars serving Austin are a fleet count Disclosed Tesla Robotaxi on X, 10 Oct 2026 →, not output.

An illustration of arithmetic, not a forecast: no one outside Tesla knows its weekly output. 125,000 a year is about 2,404 a week, and Tesla’s figure is a floor. Shares are measured against exactly 125,000, so they run high if Tesla's real capacity is above it. The 10,000-car target is a round number we chose, not a Tesla target.

What Tesla and Musk said about robotaxis and the Cybercab, and what happened.

  • 3 Missed

    Delivered

    Said 10 Oct 2024 by Elon Musk. Due 31 Dec 2026.

    “we expect to be in production with the Cybercab in probably—well, I tend to be a little optimistic with the timeframes—in 2026. So, yeah. Before 2027”

    Met inside the year; how many cars exist is still unpublished.

    Latest evidence: Tesla says more than 300 Cybercabs are serving Austin. Teslarati, 11 Oct 2026 →

    Said at: The Drive → Ledger last verified 15 Oct 2026

  2. Cybercab production to start in April 2026

    Delivered

    Said 28 Jan 2026 by Elon Musk. Due 30 Apr 2026.

    “We expect to start production in April.”

    The ledger counts CNET's 24 April report that production began.

    Latest evidence: CNET reported Cybercab production had begun, at a "very slow" initial rate. CNET, 24 Apr 2026 →

    Said at: The Motley Fool → Ledger last verified 15 Oct 2026

  3. Dedicated robotaxi in volume production in 2024

    Missed

    Said 20 Apr 2022 by Elon Musk. Due 31 Dec 2024.

    “I think can be very powerful product where we aspire to reach volume production in 2024”

    The earlier dedicated-robotaxi deadline that passed without a car.

    Latest evidence: Cybercab production began in April 2026; Musk said early production would be "very slow". CNET, 24 Apr 2026 →

  4. Over a million robotaxis on the road in 2020

    Missed

09

Questions

Buying, price, range, charging and legality, answered from the sources.

Can I buy a Cybercab?

Not yet. On the Q3 call Lars Moravy said Tesla is trying to get Cybercabs to customers by the end of 2026, and that today all of them go to Tesla's own Robotaxi fleet. Elon Musk added that "the vast majority of the Cybercab stuff will be essentially Tesla-owned and transport as a service." Tesla has published no order page, price or delivery terms.

The Motley Fool, 22 Oct 2026 →

How much will a Cybercab cost?

Tesla has not published a price. At the 2024 reveal Musk said he expected the cost to be below $30,000. That is a target from two years ago, not a quote.

The Drive, 11 Oct 2024 →

Does the Cybercab have a steering wheel or pedals?

NHTSA's audit notes the vehicles lack permanently attached, conventional manual controls such as a brake pedal, gas pedal, steering wheel and mirrors. NHTSA is examining how Tesla certified a car like that.

NHTSA, 3 Sep 2026 →

When did Cybercab rides start?

Tesla's Q3 deck says it started providing Robotaxi rides using Cybercabs on 3 September 2026 in Austin. Not a Tesla App reports public booking opened the next day.

Tesla, 21 Oct 2026 → · Not a Tesla App, 4 Sep 2026 →

How many Cybercabs are there?

Tesla says 300+ serve Austin (10 October). It has not published a production total. The >125,000 a year figure is installed capacity, which Tesla itself says is not the current production rate. For registrations and live counts use the live tracker.

Tesla Robotaxi on X, 10 Oct 2026 → · Tesla, 21 Oct 2026 →

What is the Cybercab's range and battery size?

Tesla has not published either. Car and Driver reports a battery of roughly 50 kWh and just under 280 miles from lab figures in an EPA filing, and calls them very rough estimates.

Car and Driver, 15 Jun 2026 →

How does the Cybercab charge?

The Drive reported after the 2024 reveal that the car appeared to feature wireless (inductive) charging and no traditional charge port. Not a Tesla App reports that the cars in Austin use a hidden NACS port for DC fast charging, and quotes reviewer Kyle Conner that the car "does not support AC charging". It adds that Tesla still intends to introduce wireless charging for the fleet. Tesla has not published a charging spec.

The Drive, 11 Oct 2024 → · Not a Tesla App, 5 Sep 2026 →

Is the Cybercab legal on US roads?

Tesla told NHTSA it certified the cars as compliant with all applicable federal safety standards. NHTSA opened an audit query on 3 September to examine that certification, including where Tesla decided a standard did not apply. Not a Tesla App reports Tesla's answers to 21 requests are due 30 October. An audit query is a fact-finding step, not a finding of non-compliance.

NHTSA, 3 Sep 2026 → · Not a Tesla App, 8 Oct 2026 →

Where can I ride one?

Austin, per Tesla. On the Q3 call Ashok Elluswamy, asked how the Cybercab fleet scales past Austin, said "next month, we'll be launching in Dallas", and Vaibhav Taneja said Tesla plans to bring Cybercab to other cities before the end of the year. Tesla's Robotaxi service already runs elsewhere with Model Y cars; see the live tracker for cities.

Tesla, 21 Oct 2026 → · The Motley Fool, 22 Oct 2026 →

Why does this guide say "Reported" so often?

Tesla has published no Cybercab spec sheet. Anything that comes from a news report, a filing read by a journalist or a reviewer's remark is marked Reported, never Disclosed.

Car and Driver, 15 Jun 2026 →
