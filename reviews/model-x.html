The Supercharged Daily review

Model XSpace with a sense of occasion.

Households researching a larger Tesla cabin and a particular six- or seven-seat arrangement.

Editorial buying analysis6 min guideReviewed 25 Oct 2026
The verdictDrive & characterCabinChargingSoftwareOwnershipYour checklist

The recommendation

Why this Tesla?

A distinctive used-market flagship for households that want its particular seating layout, falcon-wing access and electric performance. Choose it by the way the actual seats, doors and equipment work for your family, and it offers something much more specific than a larger Model Y.

01 / What stands out

A cabin with choices

Different seating layouts can answer very specific family requirements.

02 / What stands out

Distinctive access

Falcon-wing doors make the rear-seat experience a defining part of the car.

03 / What stands out

A connected flagship

Tesla navigation, remote preparation and charging management support the larger-cabin idea.

Read the car.

Model XFlagship SUVExplore the charging →Your comparison →

01 / Drive & character

The flagship experience, standing taller.

Model X combines an elevated cabin with the Tesla software and charging idea. Its appeal is not simply more metal for the money. It is the way a specific seating layout, access arrangement and flagship character can suit a household whose requirements are different from the five-seat crossover norm.

Model X and Model S have reached the end of their production era. Treat this review as a guide to researching individual used or available inventory cars, not an assumption that any configuration can still be ordered from the factory. Condition, generation and actual fitted equipment are central to the recommendation.

As with Model S, earlier vehicles and the refreshed dual-motor/Plaid generation differ meaningfully. Check the driving controls, infotainment and assistance hardware on the actual car. For a family vehicle, a calm test drive with your regular passengers can tell you more than choosing a variant from an acceleration chart.

02 / Cabin & daily use

The seating layout is the specification that matters most.

Model X has been offered in different seating arrangements. Five, six and seven seats are not interchangeable labels: second-row seat mechanisms, access and load flexibility differ. Verify the exact configuration, child-seat compatibility and luggage space with every intended passenger in place. A third row should be assessed by the people who will use it.

Falcon-wing doors are a defining design feature and can make rear access feel very different from a conventional SUV. Their operation is something to understand in your own surroundings. Try an appropriate demonstration with overhead and side clearances representative of where you park. Do not presume every garage is suitable or that a sensor demonstration replaces checking real clearance.

Operate every seat and powered function, inspect seals and trim, and check climate comfort across the rows. On a used car, a well-maintained interior is part of the flagship value. Bring the stroller, child seats and travel bags. The strongest Model X recommendation is one based on a layout you have personally proved works.

03 / Charging & range

The stop is part
of the journey.

Understand the charging reference, then put it in context. Peak power is useful; what the car does across the session matters more.

Check the charging source

Tesla’s North American support page lists up to 250 kW for Model X at V4 Superchargers. That family reference does not apply automatically to every production year. Older vehicle and battery generations can have different limits; check the specific example rather than assuming the newest charging description belongs to it.

A larger family SUV should be assessed by energy replenished and the distance that energy supports under your conditions. Passengers, speed, temperature and luggage can change consumption. Even at a capable site, charge rate tapers as the battery fills; a quoted maximum is not the average for an entire session.

For an older or imported example, verify connector compatibility, AC charging capability and the appropriate regional adapters or upgrades. Make the charging routine part of the purchase inspection. If home charging is available, plan cable access around the parking arrangement and the way the doors and family loading will be used.

Navigate to the stop.

Allow battery preparation. Confirm the site and the car’s expected arrival charge.

02 / Replenish

Think energy, not a spike.

kW measures power. kWh measures energy. The average over the session determines the simple time calculation.

03 / Continue

Use the onward plan.

A fuller battery generally charges more slowly. Let the car guide the charge needed for your next leg.

See the assumptions and equations

Default DC example: 75 kWh usable × (80% − 10%) = 52.5 kWh; at an assumed average 100 kW, this is 31.5 minutes. No model-specific 10–80% time is claimed.

Default home example: 60 km × 17 kWh/100 km = 10.2 kWh at the battery; at 90% efficiency, 11.33 kWh from the wall. At 7.4 kW this takes about 1.53 hours, costing £2.83 at £0.25/kWh.

Beyond the drive

The whole
experience.Software, ownership and your next step.

04 / software

A family flagship with a connected backbone.

Remote climate, account-based access, navigation and charging management support the large-cabin experience. The useful question is how the actual car fits into the family routine: who drives it, how they access it, which screens and connectivity are present, and how a journey with passengers is planned.

Hardware varies substantially over the model’s life. A later Model X’s screen arrangement or assistance capability is not a promise for an earlier example. Inspect the software and vehicle-information screens, establish which features are purchased or subscribed, and check transfer conditions before including them in the car’s value.

Driver assistance remains supervised, with feature availability depending on the hardware, software and market. Buy a cabin and a set of functions you can verify, rather than relying on a future rollout. Our evidence desk separates supervised driving from commercial driverless services; our software tracker helps owners follow the releases relevant to their installed version.

05 / ownership

Make the inspection as thorough as the specification.

Price the vehicle as the individual flagship it is. Get insurance and tyre quotes, examine service history and confirm remaining warranty. Check suspension where fitted, doors, powered seats, climate and charging. A specialist inspection is useful because the features that make Model X distinctive are also features you want working correctly throughout ownership.

For long trips, evaluate the passenger and luggage load you actually carry. Extra seats do not create extra luggage space, and roof-mounted equipment changes the energy picture. Route planning and a realistic charging reserve make more sense than assuming a brochure range describes a full family travelling at motorway speed.

Model X is most rewarding when its particular configuration solves a real household need. Compare it with Model Y using your own access and seating requirements, then decide whether the flagship experience is the one you want to keep. Choosing it for a proved fit is a stronger recommendation than awarding a generic value score against a smaller car.

The questions worth asking

A little more clarity.

Specific answers for the car you are researching.

Are all Model X cars seven-seaters?

No. Seating configurations differ, and their mechanisms and luggage arrangements matter. Check the individual car.

Is this a new-car ordering recommendation?

No. This is primarily used and available-inventory research following the end of the S/X production era.

What should a family test first?

The real seating and loading routine: child seats installed, front seats correctly positioned, all passengers seated and the travel luggage aboard.
